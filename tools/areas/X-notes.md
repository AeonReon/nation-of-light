# Writer X notes: the under-sevens — body.hopping, music.song

Both to `tools/areas/BRIEF-LITTLE.md`, matched to `food.kitchen`: twelve rungs, every rung
`ctx: "with"` with a `note` to the grown-up, `mins` on rung one only, `how` used twice in all
(the forward roll, and finding the beat), `who` = "From about four, with a grown-up beside them",
`line` rewritten to describe the last rung, `id`/`size`/`strand`/`from` untouched.

## body.hopping — Hopping and balancing (12 rungs)

**Order changed.** The old ladder did one-foot balancing and hopping (rungs 1–4) before two-footed
jumping (5–7). In a room that is backwards: a four-year-old gets both feet off the floor together
long before one foot will hold a hop. New order: stand on one foot for five · ten two-footed jumps ·
jump over a line · jump off the bottom stair · stand on one foot for ten · three hops · five hops ·
walk a line · walk it heel to toe · the forward roll · hop across the room · hop across and swap feet.
The first rung is unchanged, so the balance the hops need starts on day one and is re-tested at
rung five once the jumping has built the legs.

**The forward roll (rung 10) stays, and I think it belongs.** It is the one thing on the ladder that
is not hopping or standing, but it is the same balance from upside down, and it is an ordinary
four-to-six skill in any toddler gym class when it is done the taught way. It is only safe in one
shape, so the rung carries a four-line `how` and the longest note on the ladder: chin down on the
chest and eyes on their own tummy the whole way over, weight on the hands and the back of the
shoulders, one of the grown-up's hands behind the head holding the tuck and one at the hips. The
note says plainly: if the top of the head goes down on the floor, stop, go back to the tuck, and
leave the rung for another week if the tuck will not hold. The soft place is a folded duvet or two
quilts on carpet, in the middle of the room, **never a bed and never a sofa** — a bed is springy and
has an edge to go over. It is the only rung with no twist, deliberately: nothing on this ladder
should invite a variation on the one movement that has a neck in it. If the owner would rather not
have a roll in a phone app at all, the clean replacement is "Stand on one foot and pick something up
off the floor" — same ladder, no neck — but I would keep the roll.

**What the line is (rungs 3 and 8).** Both notes spell it out for a home with no chalk and no tape:
a skipping rope, a belt, a dressing-gown cord or a long scarf laid out straight, or something the
floor already has — the join between two floorboards or two tiles, the edge of a rug, the pattern in
the tiles, the long edge of a door mat. Rung 3 adds "lay it flat so a foot landing on it cannot roll".

**Twists.** Every existing twist kept (eyes shut on the two standing rungs, "the foot you would never
choose" on the three hopping rungs, backwards on the two line rungs, and the starting-foot twist on
rung 12). One swapped: the old "Eyes shut" on jumping over a line is now "Sideways", because a rope
underfoot plus shut eyes is the one place on this ladder where the twist makes it less safe rather
than harder. Two added: a silent landing (rung 2, which is really soft knees) and "land, then stay
still for a count of three" (rung 4).

**Unsure.** Rung 4 is capped at the bottom stair or a low doorstep and the note says nothing higher
belongs on this ladder — worth a second read, because "a low step" on its own is the sort of line a
child could take to mean a wall.

## music.song — A song all the way through (12 rungs)

**Order mostly kept, one rung reshaped.** Old rungs 2 and 3 were "on your own, nobody helping" and
then "without forgetting any of the words", which is the same rung twice with the second one phrased
as a fault. Now rung 2 is "on your own, with somebody helping only when you get stuck" and rung 3 is
"all the way through with nobody helping at all" — a real step between them, and neither of them is
about forgetting. Everything else runs as before: the beat (clap it while somebody sings, then sing
and clap together), then loud, quiet, fast and slow, then one listener, a second song, a made-up
verse, and the room.

**Rung 12, the roomful.** The test is now "Sing a song to everybody in the room at once", which is
true in a house of three — the note says so first: three people at the table after a meal, or four
faces on one video call, is a room. The gentle real version it gives is a song everybody knows,
started at the next family birthday, so the child begins on their own and the others come in after
the first line; singing it with a brother, a sister or a friend also counts. The note then sets the
rule for the grown-up: **ask privately first, never announce it to the room before they have said
yes, and if the answer is no, say that is fine, put it away and offer it again in a month or two.**
The last line is there to close the door on failure: a rung that is waiting has not been failed, and
this ladder is allowed to sit on the eleventh rung for a year. Rung 9 (one listener) is built the
same way — that person is briefed beforehand to listen to the end, say thank you, and nothing else.

**Twists** (there were none in school.json): eyes shut on remembering the words, feet instead of
hands on the beat, walking in time while singing, fast-to-slow in the middle without stopping, a
song in another language, a verse made up while singing, and on the last rung "standing up on your
own, with everybody quiet" — the harder version, kept separate from the gentle version in the note
so the two never get confused.

**`how` on rung 4 only.** Finding the beat is the one thing here a grown-up may not be able to
explain on the spot: nod to a verse until the nod is steady, clap once per nod and nothing in
between, then sing again and let the child clap with the nodding.

**Unsure.** Rung 6 (as loudly as you can) is kept before rung 7 (as quietly as only the person next
to you can hear), because loud is the easy, playful one and quiet is the control; rung 7's note says
so. If the owner would rather a shy child met the quiet one first, swapping 6 and 7 costs nothing.
