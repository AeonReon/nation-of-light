# Writer T notes: Fun and creativity, how-things-work ladders

## sky.birdsflight: Things that fly (18 rungs, no `from`; who: Everybody; a child and a grown-up side by side)
Order: a paper dart, then throwing it well, then comparing designs (averages), then what bent edges do (elevators, then ailerons), then feeling lift with a tilted card, then explaining lift, then a paper helicopter and one change to it, then a card glider built, balanced, measured (glide ratio), turned with the fin, flown in wind, then a rubber-band propeller felt and then fitted, then your own design, then a flying afternoon for others.
Dropped the old controller/drone rungs: they needed buying kit and did not follow from the rest. The far end is now all paper, card, tape and rubber bands.
Science checked: lift explained as air turned downwards / lower pressure above, and the equal-transit-time myth named as wrong. Elevator, aileron and rudder directions checked. Balance point under the front of the wing, ahead of the lift. Propeller as a spinning wing giving thrust.
Unsure: rung 16 (rubber-band propeller plane flying 3 m) is honestly fiddly; the note says so and accepts a balsa kit. Glider sizes in rung 10 are a starting point, not a tested plan.

## sky.rocket: Rockets (16 rungs, no `from`; who: From about six; a grown-up for every pressure launch)
Order: balloon on a string (the reaction idea), paper straw rocket, fins, angle, explain why a rocket moves, stamp launcher, stamp rocket height by timing, then the water-rocket safety rules BEFORE any building, then the launcher built with a grown-up, first launch, water amount, height by a 45-degree paper corner, fins and nose weight, parachute, three safe launches in a row, a launch afternoon.
Air and water pressure only; no vinegar-and-bicarbonate, no fire, no motors, and the about says none should be added. Safety: fizzy-drink bottles only (built for pressure), glasses, 5 m back, nobody over the bottle, grown-up pumps, stopper never taped or clamped (it is the safety valve), away from roads and power lines.
Science checked: Newton's third law, works in space; height ~ 5 x (time up)^2 metres, noted as an overestimate because of air drag; 45 degrees best without air, lower for light rockets; about a third water commonly best, "trust your own results".
Unsure: "at least 5 metres back" is my figure; some guides say more. The parachute rung often takes many tries.

## home.engines: How things work (16 rungs, `from: 8`; who: From about eight with a grown-up; teens and grown-ups)
Order: a clicking pen apart and back, then the take-apart safety rules, then simple machines found (lever, wedge, screw, wheel and axle), a lever tried, pulleys found, gear teeth counted, a bicycle pump's one-way washer, a clockwork toy or wind-up timer taken apart and then rebuilt, the four-stroke engine explained, a car bonnet with its owner (engine cold), find why one thing stopped, fix by one change at a time, draw from memory, teach it, mend one thing for somebody.
Take-apart rule held throughout: no plug, no charger, no battery, never microwave/TV/screens/power supplies; capacitors named as the reason; big springs named. Fridge, cistern, tap, lock, zip, switch left to the daily extras; circuits left to make.electric.
Science checked: levers/wheel and axle trade force for distance; gear ratio = teeth divided; four strokes; diesel ignites by compression; one-way valve in the pump.
Unsure: rung 11 needs a car owner; the note offers a labelled handbook drawing as the no-car way. The clockwork rebuild (rung 9) can be hard; rung 8 says let the spring run down and wear glasses.

## home.bike: Bike mechanics (19 rungs, `from: 8`; who: From about eight with a grown-up; teens and grown-ups)
Order: read the pressure on the tyre before pumping (the old order had pumping first), pump, pre-ride check, saddle height, chain clean and oil, chain back on, front wheel out and in (easier), back wheel, tyre off and on, find the hole, patch (checked overnight, so `long`), spare tube away from home, brake pad wear, brake barrel adjust, pads refitted, gear adjust, trace a noise, revive a long-unused bike, teach a puncture.
Grown-up brake check written into every rung that touches wheels or brakes, and in the about. Hydraulic disc brakes sent to a bike shop; limit screws left alone. Dropped "true a wheel" and "strip and rebuild a part" (need special tools and skill beyond three months).
Checked: barrel adjuster anticlockwise tightens the cable (brakes: pads closer; rear gears: towards bigger sprockets). Heel-on-pedal saddle method. Chain oil not cooking oil. Rim brake quick-release or V-brake pipe opened before removing a wheel.
Unsure: rung 13's disc-pad limit ("thinner than the metal plate behind it") is a rough rule; the note points to the maker's figure. Rung 16 needs a bike with outside gears.

## make.electric: Electricity (18 rungs, `from: 8`; who: From about eight with a grown-up; teens and grown-ups)
Order: trace the loop inside an AA torch, then the four safety rules, then light the bulb with kitchen foil, draw it, conductors and insulators, a paper-clip switch, two batteries (voltage adds), series and parallel, electromagnet, water-pipe explanation, meter volts, meter continuity, LED resistor worked out, breadboard, dark-activated light, microcontroller blink (real board or free simulator), button and sensor, a gadget for somebody kept working a month.
Safety: 9 V max, mains kills said plainly in the about and rule one; never lithium/rechargeable cut, pierced or shorted; button batteries away from small children; electromagnet on one ordinary AA for a few seconds. Soldering removed completely, because a soldering iron is a mains tool (the old ladder had two soldering rungs); breadboard and screwless building instead.
Science checked: series shares voltage and both dim, parallel each full brightness and battery drains faster; LED needs a resistor, (9-2)/0.02 = 350, next size up; tap water and pencil lead conduct weakly; electromagnet coil nearly a short circuit.
Unsure: rung 3 lights a white torch LED from two AA with no resistor; normally fine for a moment with alkaline cells, but a red LED could be damaged. Rungs 11 onwards need a meter, parts or a board; the notes give borrowing and free simulators (Wokwi named) as the no-spend way.

## make.machines: renamed "Making things with code" (20 rungs, no `from`; who: From about eight, a grown-up nearby online; grown-ups too)
Renamed because the ladder has no machines in it: it is code, from blocks to a live web page. Order: exact instructions on paper (no computer), online safety rules, Scratch/code.org with no account (move, loop, if, variable, finished game), then typed code (HTML page, CSS, a button in JavaScript, errors in the console), plan a list page on paper, build it, localStorage, ask an AI to explain one line, phone width, free hosting, watch a user, fix and republish without losing the working copy, add what they asked for and see them use it on three days.
Online safety: grown-up owns any account for a child and reads its age rules; no name, school, address or photo anywhere; AI tools and hosting often 13+ or 18+ so a grown-up's account; never paste passwords.
Dropped the "add two numbers" rung (the list page covers input) and the three idea-talking rungs (replaced by the paper-plan rung before the list).
Unsure: named free sites (Scratch, code.org, MDN, freeCodeCamp, GitHub Pages, Netlify, Wokwi) could change their rules; the notes do not promise more than "free" and "usually 13 or older". Last rung depends on another person choosing to use it, as the old one did.
