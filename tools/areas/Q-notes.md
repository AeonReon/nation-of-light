# Writer Q notes: Fun and creativity (music)

Kept clear of `music.play1` (its whistle-a-tune, clap-back, dance-alone and five-note rungs are single tastes; these ladders go further), `music.sing` (warm-up, note matching, rounds, starting a group song) and `words.poems` (four rhyming lines, eight lines): songwriting starts from a tune and a chorus, not a poem. None of the six had a `twist`, so none to keep. No `from` set: every ladder works from the age in its `who`, with a grown-up alongside.

## music.whistle: Whistling (12 rungs; who: from about six, side by side)
Order: any sound → one clear held note → pitch moved by the tongue → a slow slide over the range → five scale notes against a piano app → a known tune → somebody names it → in tune with a recording → three from memory → whistling on the in-breath → finger whistle → teach somebody. Size is "afternoon", so 12; the far end is short of three months for most.
Unsure: the finger whistle rung is `home` (it is loud; the note says outside or where noise does not matter, never near an ear). Some people never get a pucker whistle quickly; rung 1 says it can take days. The tongue-forward-raises-pitch explanation is the common description, stated gently.

## music.rhythm: Rhythm (16 rungs, up from 12; who: from about six, side by side)
Order: clap to a song → count 1–4 → with a metronome → silent-metronome check → record yourself for drift → clap the rhythm of words (rhythm vs beat) → foot on beat + hands on words → clap back a rhythm (with; a free video as the alone option) → offbeat → three vs four ("Happy Birthday" is in three, "Twinkle" in four) → two hands → a basic drum pattern on a table → with a whole song → rest four bars and come back on 1 → hold the beat under a singer → lead a group that stops on a signal.
The old drum rungs (`kit`) are now a table and hands, so almost all are `room`.

## music.dance: Dance (20 rungs; who: for everybody, side by side)
Order: step on the beat → one basic step from a video → a song without counting → two steps switched every eight → a short group dance with the video → without it → keep going through mistakes → record and tidy one move → for one person → an unknown song → a dance from another part of the world (a week) → make up your own (4×8) → the same on two days → learn one alongside somebody → a partner basic step holding hands → lead and follow → join in where others dance (out) → teach your dance to one → to two or three → teach and lead five or more.
Dropped the old ceili set dances (one country), "ask somebody to dance" and "dance with somebody much older" (both could read as a joke or romance). Styles named from several places (line dance, Balkan/Greek circle dance, Bollywood routine, West African class, Scottish/Irish folk, merengue, Korean pop, waltz, salsa, swing). Safety line on rung 1 (clear space, no socks on a smooth floor).
Unsure: rung 17 needs a place where people dance; the note gives a family party as the easy route.

## music.instrument: An instrument (20 rungs; who: from about seven, side by side)
Order: get one and one clear note → tune it with a free tuner app → three named notes → five up and down → "Ode to Joy" / "Mary Had a Little Lamb" on five notes → three times clean → in time with a metronome → ten minutes a day for a week → a second tune alone → fix the bar you get wrong → three from memory → one tune for one person → a tune by ear → a tune from the page or tab → thirty days → beginner version of a song you like → through three times → from memory → with somebody singing along → for two or three people who sing along. Line and about now match that end.
Checked: Ode to Joy E E F G G F E D C C D E E D D and Mary E D C D E E E, D D D, E G G both fit in C–G.
Unsure: rung 1 is `kit` (an instrument is a specific object) even though a phone piano app works; the app would find it only in the needs fold.

## music.readmusic: Reading music (20 rungs; who: from about eight, side by side)
Order: find C on a keyboard app → the letters C to C → treble spaces FACE → lines EGBDF → middle C and C–G written and played → a note-naming quiz (18/20) → crotchets and minims counted → semibreves and rests → quavers ("1 and 2 and") → time signatures → dotted notes in 3/4 → first eight bars of Ode to Joy from the page → an unheard eight-bar tune → sharps, flats, naturals → a key signature of one sharp → repeats and first/second endings → bass clef lines GBDFA, spaces ACEG, middle C above → a left-hand bass line → write your own four bars and have it played back → sight-read an unseen tune for somebody.
Old order put naming every note before learning the clef, and middle C at rung 8 after playing tunes; fixed. All playing is on a free piano app, so `room` throughout except the last.
Facts checked: treble G clef curls round line 2; bass F clef dots round line 4; middle C one ledger line below treble / above bass; accidentals last to the end of the bar; key-signature F sharp sits on the treble top line; one sharp = G major / E minor; dotted note = half as long again; Ode to Joy bar 4 = dotted crotchet, quaver, minim. British note names with the other name in brackets (quarter note etc.) because both are in use worldwide.

## music.songwrite: Writing a song (20 rungs; who: from about eight, side by side)
Order: new words to a known tune → one-sentence subject → eight plain lines → the best line into a four-line chorus → fit the chorus to a clapped beat → a tune for it, recorded → sing it from memory next day → verse one → its own tune → verse two → cut → full draft under three minutes → sing to one person, ask which line they remember → change one line → add a beat or three chords → write it out with headings → a second song about somebody you know (a week, kind lines only) → give it to them → teach the chorus to two or more → sing for three or more who join in on the last chorus.
Old rung 15 ("write a whole song and perform it", `out`) cut: it jumped ahead and needed a venue. No rung needs an instrument.
Unsure: rung 16 mentions writing the chorus on a stave "if you read music"; optional, not required.
