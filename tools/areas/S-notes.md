# Writer S notes: Fun and creativity, movement and play

Kept clear of Health: no squat, press-up, plank, stretch or running rungs (fit.body1, fit.stretch, fit.squat, body.run); no hopping-for-under-sevens rungs (body.hopping). Balance's one-leg bend (rung 8) is a small controlled knee bend holding a bottle, not the fit.squat one-leg squat.

## body.juggle: Juggling (20 rungs, `from: 8`; who: From about eight, and grown-ups)
Order kept almost as it was, because the early rungs were good: one ball (same height, eyes on top, weaker hand), two-ball exchange (5, 10, weaker hand first, feet still), the first three-ball throw, the flash of three, then 5 catches, ten minutes' practice, 10, 20, 30 s, 1 min, two in one hand, walking, teaching, and two minutes last so the line and the goal (three balls) match.
Changes: the ten-minute practice moved from 15 to 12 (it is where the drops are) and its ctx corrected from `long` to `home` (one sitting). Rungs 5+ are `home` (rolled socks and a clear space), not `kit`. Teaching now has a clear end (five exchanges without you).
Unsure: `from: 8` hides it from younger children; a keen six-year-old can do the one-ball rungs. No twists existed.

## body.skip: Skipping (16 rungs, no `from`; who: Everybody; a child and a grown-up side by side)
Order: quiet jumps with no rope, setting rope length, swing and land, step over, single jumps, 3, 10, 25, a timed minute, 50, the running step, one foot, 100, one criss-cross, running into a long rope, turning a long rope for somebody else (last, matches the line).
Safety: rope never round a neck, put away from small children alone; ceiling and light check indoors.
Unsure: the entry timing for long rope ("just after it touches the ground and goes up away from you") is how it is usually taught, but traditions vary. Rope length guide (armpits; 2.5 m grown-up / 2 m child) is a rule of thumb.

## body.balance: Balance (20 rungs, no `from`; who: Everybody; a grown-up beside a young child outdoors)
Order: floor first (one foot 30 s, 1 min, heel-to-toe forwards then backwards, pick up a sock, eyes shut, toe taps round, a small knee bend holding a bottle, heel-to-toe eyes shut, a cushion), then outdoors on a log, low wall or a plank on the ground (walk, stop, backwards, one foot on it, turn in the middle, crouch), then something narrower (walk, stop), teach, and the last rung is narrow + turn + back.
Twists kept (4): "The foot you would never choose." on rungs 1, 2, 5 (old 2, 3, 9) and "The other leg." on rung 8 (old 10, now a gentler bend holding something).
Dropped: the kerb "the length of a street" (beside traffic) and the slackline as a required object (it costs money and needs rigging); slackline is allowed as an option on rung 20.
`place`: the current ladder had NO `place` on any rung (the task said some did). I ADDED `"place": "outdoors"` to rungs 11 to 20, every rung that needs a log, wall or beam outside. Every outdoor rung says no higher than knee height with soft ground beside. Remove the tags if you would rather they still appear in the needs fold.

## body.upside: Upside down (20 rungs, no `from`; who: Everybody; a grown-up close by for a child)
Order: rock on a round back, forward roll on something soft, three in a line, from standing with no hands, bunny kicks, feet-on-sofa plank, pike with feet on sofa, wall walk to an L, chest-to-wall walk-up 10 s then 30 s, cartwheel any shape, straight legs, other side, the step-out (turn a hand and come down sideways) BEFORE any back-to-wall kick-up, back-to-wall 10 s and 30 s, free kick-up 1 s, teach the roll, free 3 s, and the last rung is roll + cartwheel + 3 s handstand.
Safety: about says soft floor, warm wrists, stop if anything hurts, ask a doctor with neck/back/wrist/shoulder problems, high blood pressure or eye trouble; no headstands, nothing on the neck, headstands for children only with a trained coach.
Dropped: the backward roll (it loads the neck, and doing it well usually needs a coach or a slope), the old "come out of a handstand into a roll" (replaced by the sideways step-out), handstand walking (months past three), and the spotting rung "hold their legs while they try" (spotting a handstand badly is a hazard; teaching the forward roll instead).
Unsure: a free three-second handstand at about three months is realistic for most children and fit adults who practise most days, less so for an unfit adult starting from nothing. No twists existed.

## sky.throw: Throwing (14 rungs, no `from`; who: Everybody; a child and a grown-up side by side)
Order: paper ball into a bin (once, then 5/5), wall catch alone, catching from a partner, overarm at a bin-lid target from 10 paces (once, 5/5), 20 paces once, other hand 3/5, a catch-and-throw rally, a long throw beaten by five paces, skimming 3 and 6 bounces, 4/5 from 20 paces, and teaching the overarm throw last (no water needed to finish).
Twists kept (8), all on rungs that survive in a similar form: bin 1 and 2, catch (now 4, "One hand, the other one."), target 10 paces (5), 5/5 (6), 20 paces (7), far throw (10, "Other hand, then beat that too."), skim three (11).
Dropped: skim ten bounces and the "teach skimming" ending, so the last rung does not depend on water. The two skim rungs carry `place: "water"`.
Unsure: whether rung 3's wall is easy to find in a flat with no garden (a park ball wall is offered).

## sky.kite: Kites (12 rungs, no `from`; who: Everybody; a grown-up with a child when flying)
Order: read the wind from a window (the room-only first rung, 2 min), make a paper-and-straw sled kite, find the wind direction outside, fly one minute, launch alone, land alone, ten minutes, make a bin-bag kite with two sticks, fly it with gloves, change one thing and compare, 30 m of line for ten minutes, teach two children (last, as before).
Safety: open space, never near power lines, roads, railways or airports, not in rain or thunder, let go and keep away if it touches a power line, never wrap line round a hand, leave a kite that lands somewhere high; height limits vary by country, so the reader checks theirs.
The current ladder had no `kit` field, only `ctx: "kit"` on some rungs; now both home-made kites are free, so those are `home` (making) and `out` (flying).
Unsure: the paper sled template is described in words only ("a house turned on its side"); a first-timer might want a picture. The 10 to 25 km/h wind range is a rough guide for light sled kites.
