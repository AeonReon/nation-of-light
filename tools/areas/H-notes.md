# Writer H notes

## belong.shops: Fine shops (12 rungs, from 10; grown-ups and teenagers, a child with a grown-up)
Order: one shop looked up at home, then in and out saying nothing, then "just looking today, thank you" when offered help, one question, the difference between two prices, asking to hold one thing (saying honestly you are not buying), a second kind of shop, what staff would choose, going back a second time, how the best version is made, half an hour at ease, and last taking somebody who would never have gone in. Honesty is built in: nobody pretends to be a buyer, "just looking today" is the line throughout.
Kept clear of belong.chair (prices looked up from home, phoning places) and deal.price.
Unsure: rung 1 assumes a fine shop is reachable; the note gives "the best shop of any kind you can reach" for rural readers.

## belong.cars: Cars (12 rungs, from 10; grown-ups and teenagers, a child with a grown-up)
Order: one car looked up (price, what drives it, where made), a year of running costs, one seen up close somewhere free, a showroom look with "just looking today", one question, sitting in it (saying you are not buying), what owners like and wish were different, a different kind of car place, how one part works, on paper what owning it would honestly take, an honest test drive, and last taking somebody for their first look.
Unsure: the test drive (rung 11). Asking for one while years from buying could read as pretending, so the rung makes the reader say plainly how far away they are, and accepts a no. It needs a licence; non-drivers ask for a passenger ride or to sit with the engine running. Some showrooms will refuse all of that; the note says both answers are fair. Dropped "walk in late on purpose"-type provocations; nothing here tricks a salesperson.

## belong.rooms: Grand rooms (12 rungs, from 8; a child with a grown-up everywhere)
Order: the going-in that follows belong.chair. Look up at one ceiling, find three details, ask staff one question, a free guided tour (counts if booked on belong.chair), ask at a grand hotel's reception whether non-residents may sit in the lounge, a free talk or lunchtime concert, speak to one person after it, the public seats at a council meeting or court, an exhibition opening that says open to all, an hour alone in a grand building (a library reading room), the story of one room, and last an evening event in the grandest room near you, stayed to the end.
Removed from the old ladder: using the lavatory in a grand building (reads as a non-customer taking a service) and sitting in a hotel lobby unasked (edges into pretending to be a guest); the lounge rung now asks first. Coffee somewhere smart and eating alone in a good restaurant went too (spending; belong.chair already books a visit like that).
Unsure: court public galleries and children. Many courts do not admit children, so the note sends a child to the council meeting instead. Rules vary a lot by country; the note says check first.

## belong.carry: Carrying yourself (20 rungs, from 10; a child and a grown-up side by side)
Order: the physical habits at home first (stand one minute, walk five times unhurried, sit properly), then taken out (walk into a shop, stand evenly for a conversation, look at the face while speaking, say your name clearly), then words (thank you instead of sorry for the trouble, a compliment with just thank you, a day of only real sorries, asking as a plain sentence, a three-second pause), then harder rooms (something you own and feel good in, a place you feel out of place, asking staff there, introducing yourself to whoever runs a gathering, disagreeing calmly with somebody older or in charge, welcoming somebody newer), then one whole day of all of it and last seven days with a line each evening.
Dropped "walk in late on purpose" (rude to others) and "introduce yourself to the most senior person" became "the person running it", which a child can do too.
Unsure: eye contact and handshakes differ by culture; both notes say do what is respectful where you are. No posture claims are made about health or how others see you.

## money.earn: Earning (20 rungs, from 8; a child only with a grown-up's say-so)
Order: five jobs people pay for, one job agreed (what, when, how much) before starting, done and shown, an earnings page, asking what to do better, finding the usual price and setting yours, saying it plainly, a job outside the household (child: families the grown-up knows only, never into a house alone), on time three jobs running, a note of what you offer (child: grown-up's number, never own details, never online), handed to five people or a permitted noticeboard, one new job from it, going back a week later to put things right free, the same job four times on a regular day, another job from a pleased customer, real pay per hour including travel, a higher price for new customers, a two-person job with the share agreed and paid, half saved for four weeks, and last three regular customers kept for twelve weeks.
Kept clear of money.value1 (skill as an offer, a tenth put aside, three people paying for the same thing, one-page plan), money.run and money.sell.
Unsure: child labour and tax rules differ everywhere; the about says there may be age and hours laws for working for a business, and the last rung tells a grown-up to check their national tax office. Twelve weeks of three regular customers is about right for three months but may take a child longer.

## money.sell: Selling (20 rungs, from 8; a grown-up beside a child for every sale)
Order: one sentence (what, who for, price), said until fluent, offered to one person you know, three more with yeses and nos written down, asking what they want before offering, a sales page with costs, ten new people, asking after a no what would have made it right, changing the product from that, a two-hour table at an event where selling is allowed with a float, counting the takings against the page, telling a customer something honest that might stop the sale, asking why they bought, using their words, a stated promise for unhappy customers, a repeat sale, asking for a word-of-mouth mention, twenty different buyers, helping somebody make their first sale, and last thirty sales of the same thing with five repeat buyers.
Safety: children never sell online or to strangers alone; grown-ups selling online hand over in a busy public place and keep their home address private. Sell only what you made, grew or own with the owner's say-so, and only where local rules allow (street selling often needs permission).
Unsure: "a float" is explained in the rung as coins and notes for change; check the owner is happy with the word. Repeat customers depend on something people buy more than once, which is why the first rung's examples are plants, biscuits and bookmarks.
