# Writer U notes: New experiences (sky, living things, maps, finding your way, shore, bike)

## Who owns what
- `sky.wind` = watching the sky YOURSELF (wind, clouds, pressure, your own calls, a diary). `wild.weather` owns forecasts, warnings, tides and cold signs; sky.wind only points at it. Rung 17 (a week of evening calls, scored) says it is the week-long version of wild.door1 rung 6. Rung 7 (five cloud types over a week) and rung 5 (the three families) go past the `x-name-clouds` extra and say to count it.
- `wild.living` = trees and birds only (no wild plants, which drifted towards foraging). Rungs 2 and 9 say to go past the `x-street-trees` / `x-street-birds` extras. Dawn listening (rung 13) is by ear at first light, not the `x-listen-dusk` sound list.
- `mind.maps` = READING maps (phone, paper, key, scale, grids, lat/long, contours, turning the map by streets, planning). `wild.wayfind` = getting across the ground: sun, shadow stick, watch, compass, bearings, step count, handrails, aiming off, poor light. Turning the map is by streets in Maps (rung 7) and by compass in Finding your way (rung 11); each note says so.

## sky.wind: Wind and weather watching (20 rungs; who: everybody, side by side)
Order: wind direction by feel, strength by what it moves, a streamer, a week of morning readings, the three cloud families, cloud movement against a fixed point, five types over a week, a two-week diary, then signs tested one at a time (first heap cloud, a growing shower cloud, thunder distance, high cloud thickening, the crossed-winds rule, a week of pressure), a month of diary, reading the month back, a scored week of own calls, own call vs forecast, a local sign tested, teaching somebody. Roughly three months.
Both hemispheres: the crossed-winds rule is given both ways (north: clouds from the left = worse; south: from the right). About says the signs are mid-latitude and near the equator the diary finds the pattern.
Unsure: rung 11 waits on a thunderstorm and rung 12 on the right sky (both say go on and come back). Rung 14 needs a phone with a pressure sensor or the weather service's station readings; many cheaper phones have no sensor. Crossed-winds rule is stated as "more likely", not settled.

## wild.living: Trees and birds (20 rungs; who: everybody, side by side)
Order: fallen leaves compared, one tree named with an app and checked, adopt "your tree" (visited weekly to the end), five then ten trees without help (age guess from girth between them), trees with no leaves, then birds: a ten-minute count, the five commonest, describing an unknown one, ten on sight, three by call, the dawn chorus, a water dish for two weeks, five by ear, nests watched from a distance, a month's list, four weekly tree visits, the tree's record read back, a walk showing somebody ten.
Rules baked in: leaves only from the ground, never pick or dig up plants (many protected), never touch a nest or take eggs (against the law in many countries), "what is common where you live" not named species. Apps named: Seek, Pl@ntNet, Merlin Bird ID (all free, worldwide).
Unsure: the girth-to-age guide (about 2.5 cm round the trunk per year, open-grown) is a rough rule some tree groups use; the note calls it a guess. Nesting months given as roughly March–July north / August–December south, rains in warm countries. Rung 7 is seasonal where trees drop leaves.

## mind.maps: Maps (17 rungs, size weeks; who: from about eight with a grown-up out of doors; grown-ups)
Order: phone dot and street names, satellite view, get a paper map (free sources), the key, the scale bar checked against the phone, a timed one-kilometre walk, turning the map by the streets, a new one-kilometre walk on paper alone, grid squares, six-figure reference found by somebody else, latitude and longitude sent to somebody (with the emergency use), contour lines, choosing the gentler way up a hill and walking it, drawing your streets from memory, planning a five-kilometre walk with times, walking it, planning and leading a day out.
Dropped the old timetable rungs (brief says maps). Added `place: "hill"` on rung 13 (the walk up a hill), matching body.ride's hill rung, so it never comes up as a home pick; drop it if new rung-level places are not wanted.
Unsure: grid references are not universal; rungs 9–10 give the letter-number version and rung 10 points to lat/long (rung 11) for maps with no grid. Example coordinates are the Eiffel Tower (48.858, 2.294).

## wild.wayfind: Finding your way (20 rungs; who: grown-ups and teenagers; children from ten with a grown-up)
Order: no tools (sunrise/sunset directions, landmarks, the turns of a known route), then the sun (midday direction, shadow stick, watch), then the compass (four points, a bearing to something seen and back, step count, the triangle), compass with map (turning the map, a bearing from the map to an unseen place), walkers' methods (handrail and catching feature, aiming off, saying where you are with three reasons), dusk or mist on known ground, a written route plan, walking it with the plan left at home, teaching a bearing, leading a half-day route.
Both hemispheres as asked: midday sun in the south north of the tropics, in the north south of them, poor between; the watch method both ways plus the summer-time 1 o'clock; shadow stick works everywhere; June/December sunrise directions true in both halves. Magnetic difference "a few degrees in some places, more than ten in others", from the map edge.
Unsure: no `from` set (the early rungs suit younger children too; who carries the age). Dropped the old "change the route because of weather" rung as wild.weather rung 18 does it. Rung 16 is ctx `with` (somebody beside you, by design).

## water.pools: Rock pools and shore (12 rungs, size afternoon, ladder `place: "coast"` kept; who: everybody, a grown-up always with a child at the water)
Order: low water time (points to wild.weather for tides in full; notes seas with almost no tide), kit and a turn-back time, watch one pool untouched, five creatures by looking, a stone lifted and put back, one creature in a tub and returned to its pool, five named, high shore vs low shore, the strandline, ten pieces of rubbish, a written count of one pool, leading a hunt for younger children.
Safety: grip shoes, walk on rough rock not seaweed, arm's reach of young children, never pull limpets/anemones/starfish off, one creature at a time, ten minutes max, shade; warm seas have dangerous stingers so look only there; washed-up jellyfish still sting; leave needles/drums and report them.
Unsure: rung 10 (rubbish) is new, not in the brief's list; it is a kindness rung that fits the "leave it as you found it" thread. Egg-case descriptions (skate/ray/shark purses, whelk-type egg masses) are general; shores differ.

## body.ride: Riding a bike (18 rungs, size weeks; who: anybody learning, a grown-up with any child near roads)
Renamed from "Riding" to "Riding a bike": "Riding" alone could mean a horse. Revert if names must not change.
Order: helmet fit, bike check (tyres, brakes, seat low), sit and walk it along, brakes before gliding, glide three seconds, glide ten with a curve, pedal ten metres alone, start from standing, stop on a line, zigzag, look back while riding straight (old note kept in spirit), one-arm signal, a hill (rung `place: "hill"` kept), three kilometres of traffic-free path, learn your country's road rules, a quiet road loop with an experienced rider, ride to a real place, a planned ten-kilometre ride.
Safety: grown-up with any child on or near roads; rules differ by country (side of road, pavements, helmet law, lights); helmets after a crash replaced; grown-up holds shoulders not the seat. Rungs 3–10 say a rider can do them in one session and count them.
Unsure: "the left pedal unscrews the opposite way" is true for standard bikes. Rungs 17–18 are ctx `out` (an adult does them alone; the note puts a grown-up with a child).
