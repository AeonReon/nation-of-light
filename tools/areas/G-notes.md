# Writer G notes

## nerve.phone: On the phone (12 rungs, `from: 10`, a child calls businesses with a grown-up in the room)
Order: ring somebody you know instead of messaging → written first sentence → notes and a name during a call → a clear voicemail → a booking read back → change a detail on something you already have → menu and hold → ring a named stranger about joining a group → report a problem and get a reference → ring back on the due date → call on behalf of somebody → every put-off call in one sitting.
Kept clear of `speak.people1` rungs 10–11 (phone a shop with a simple question / a slightly bigger ask) and `deal.price` (asking who can decide), so no "does the shop have it" and no "ask for the manager" rung.
Unsure: rung 10 (ring back on the due date) depends on rung 9 not being sorted; the note covers it (ring to say thanks and check it is closed). Rung 11 (calling on behalf of somebody): some banks and services will only talk to the account holder, and the note says to keep them close enough to take the phone.

## nerve.walkin: Walking in (14 rungs, `from: 10`, a child's grown-up meets the leader and waits nearby)
Order: a free public building → a shop never entered → sit alone twenty minutes → a free public event alone → sit next to somebody → find a weekly group → first visit, saying it is your first → stay for the tea → go back within two weeks → walk into a drop-in already under way → a gathering where you know one person → a group where you are the youngest or oldest → four weeks in a row → bring somebody new.
Took the "sit alone in a café" rung here, so it is not in `nerve.odd`. No ordering at counters (`speak.strangers`) and no chatting-up rungs (`speak.people1`).
Unsure: overlaps a little with `speak.people2` rung 7 (same regular thing twice in a week) and rung 18 (go somewhere alone where you know nobody). The focus here is getting through the door, not the people. Rung 12 (youngest/oldest in the room) is harmless, but check it reads kindly.

## nerve.no: Getting told no (12 rungs, `from: 10`, a child asks strangers only with a grown-up beside them)
Order: write down asks you never made → a no at home answered with "fair enough" → write down what you feared vs what happened → ask for a look behind the scenes → three noes in a week → ask for the reason → ask what would make it a yes → ask again after something changed → put yourself forward where you might not be picked → a three-step plan after a no → twenty noes in four weeks → apply for something most people are turned down for.
The ladder's rule, stated in `about` and in rungs 5/6/11: a no is always allowed, one friendly question, then leave it, and only ask for things where a yes would be fine for them too. Avoided `nerve.asking` rung 11 (ask the person who can say yes) by making the late rungs about being picked or selected, not asking a person.
Unsure: rung 4 (a behind-the-scenes look) might irritate a busy place; the note says "at a quiet time". Twenty noes in four weeks is a guess at "about three months of a little most days".

## nerve.onthespot: Speaking up on the spot (12 rungs, `from: 8`, early rungs at home)
Order: say one thing at the family table unasked → speak into a pause → topic game for 30 seconds → the shape (one point, one example, stop) → one minute in the game → honest answers on subjects you know little about → add to somebody's point by name → go first in a round → offer your own idea when a group decides → sum up a discussion → thirty seconds of thanks standing → two minutes standing to ten people on a subject handed to you.
Kept clear of `nerve.asking` rung 5 (a question in front of a group), `speak.hardthing` (disagreeing, "I don't understand", saying no), and `speak.stage` (prepared talks).
Unsure: rung 12 mentions public speaking clubs where guests can try it free. Toastmasters-type clubs exist in most countries and usually let guests try, but not everywhere, so the note offers a family gathering as well.

## nerve.odd: Not minding what people think (12 rungs, everybody, no `from`)
Order, from least to most noticeable: hum → stop and look at something for two minutes → wear something you like → sing a song outdoors → play in a park → draw in public → first to clap → cheer strangers at a race → dance when others are → first to join in → list three things put off and do the smallest → the biggest, weekly for a month.
Every rung is kind, harmless and cheerful. Safety lines: not in quiet places, not in anybody's way, never take a child's turn on a swing, only where allowed. The `about` says to do it alone another day if it would embarrass the person you are with. Dropped the old "harmlessly odd in public", "too cool for it" (might embarrass teenagers around you), and "ask an obvious question" (that belongs to group speaking).
Unsure: rung 5 (a grown-up on a climbing frame). Some playgrounds are for children only, which the note covers, but check it sits right with a family app.

## speak.stage: Standing up in front of people (20 rungs, `from: 8`, a grown-up arranges a child's audiences)
Order: read a page aloud to an empty room → record and listen → read to one or two people → stand still → three points on a card → one minute alone → one minute to people → first and last sentences → two minutes to three people → a three-second pause → a voice that reaches the back of a hall → read to a group outside home → plan and practise five minutes → practise carrying on through interruptions → five minutes to five people plus questions → ask for feedback and change it → offer a ten-minute talk → ten minutes to fifteen people → plan fifteen minutes and try it at home → fifteen minutes to thirty people with questions.
Dropped the old "welcome circle" and "day out" rungs (those assume the school's own events), plus the unplanned "I do not know" and "on the spot" material (now in `nerve.onthespot`).
Unsure: thirty people in about three months is realistic through a club, an assembly or a society, but it depends on somebody inviting you. Rung 20's note points back to the rung 17 group. Rung 11 needs a hall or a big space; the note gives a garden or open doors as the no-hall version.
