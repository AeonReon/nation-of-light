# Writer F notes

## calm.still — Sitting still (18 rungs, from 12, "Grown-ups and anybody from about twelve, after part one")
Order: made it the longer, sitting-only practice that follows Stillness, part one (which ends at twenty minutes plus gratitude and the hard thing first); `about` says so and rung 1 sends true beginners to part one. Ten minutes on a chair, then finding a posture you can hold for twenty, five days a week, then methods one at a time (breath at one spot, noting "thinking", three breaths before moving, listening), twenty-five, twenty in public noise, thirty once, fourteen mornings, a reluctant morning, slow walking, sit-walk-sit (fifty minutes), thirty for seven mornings, forty-five once, leading five minutes for others, and thirty mornings of thirty minutes.
Avoided: part one's breath counting, beautiful-thing looking and sitting with a hard feeling.
Unsure: safety is covered by "stop if it is too much, talk to somebody you trust" in `about`, pain/numbness notes, and "rest if ill"; no claims about benefits. The noting method is attributed to "some Buddhist traditions" and slow walking to "many traditions" — both true but general. The leading rung (17) is `with`; there is no `after: calm.still1` gate (not in the brief to add one) — worth considering.

## classic.think — Thinking on your own (16 rungs, from 10, "Grown-ups and anybody from about ten")
Order: five minutes with a question said aloud → ten minutes and three written sentences → a phone-free walk → write what you think before looking up → a whole page → read somebody else and compare → test a much-repeated idea for and against → the thinking notebook starts (four weekly pages) → change your mind on paper → twenty minutes with a pen → an unplanned screen-free hour → a practical thing you avoid → a decision on paper → saying an unpopular view calmly → a screen-light day → twelve weekly pages read back. The notebook is the spine so the last rung is about three months after rung 8.
Avoided: mind.learn1's thirty minutes with a pen (this is twenty), belong.story's self-limiting sentences (rung 7 uses ideas about the world, not about yourself), still1's good-life page.
Unsure: rung 12 steers people to practical avoided things and away from grief/pain, on purpose. Rung 3 walk has a phone-for-safety and child-route line.

## classic.stories — The great stories (18 rungs, no from, "For everybody; a child and a grown-up side by side")
Order: one fable → its lesson → three fables from three places (Aesop, Panchatantra, La Fontaine/Krylov) → a trickster (Anansi, Coyote, Reynard, Monkey King) → learn one to tell → tell it → creation stories from three peoples → a hero story (Sundiata, Mulan, Momotarō, Theseus) → one story in two far-apart places (Ye Xian/Cinderella, Gilgamesh/Noah) → the pattern in something recent → a whole epic in a retelling (Odyssey, Ramayana, Gilgamesh, Sundiata, Journey to the West, Kalevala, Popol Vuh) → retell it in five minutes → which character you are like → a people you know nothing about → a legend of your own area → read aloud with voices → learn two more to tell → three stories, three parts of the world, from memory, one sitting.
Sensitivity: Genesis and Noah appear beside other traditions; the note says some stories are sacred to people alive today and to describe them "the way the tradition tells them". Deliberately left out Br'er Rabbit (the Uncle Remus collection is contested).
Facts to check if wanted: Ye Xian "written down more than a thousand years ago" (Duan Chengshi, about 850–860); Rangi and Papa separated by their children; Pangu and the egg; Ymir; Krylov as a fable writer; "many libraries have free audio versions".

## classic.big — The big questions (19 rungs, no from, "From about ten; younger children with a grown-up")
Order: kitchen-table questions (good, lying with the best case for the other side, fair, the Ship of Theseus, same person at five, unseen beauty) → write three answers fairly without choosing (training for the traditions) → ask an older person about a good life → read one tradition's own words → write it so a follower would agree → a second tradition from another part of the world → where they agree and differ, undecided → ask somebody from a different tradition and listen → the best case for a view you do not hold → a week with one question → your own page, with where your view came from → have it questioned kindly → a big question with a child under eight → run a half-hour conversation where every view gets a fair hearing.
Neutrality: sources listed are the texts themselves (Analects, Dhammapada, Epictetus's Handbook, Proverbs / Sermon on the Mount, the Qur'an, Bhagavad Gita, Tao Te Ching; Ubuntu described as "often put as"). No rung asks the reader to pick a winner; rung 17 lets them keep their page unchanged. The third-question rung about what happens after death was dropped as too heavy for somebody grieving.
Unsure: "Plutarch nearly two thousand years ago" (Life of Theseus, about AD 100 — fine). Rung 18 needs access to a young child; note gives the relative's/friend's-child option with the parent there. No `from` because the first eight rungs work for young children with a grown-up; `who` says so.

## calm.hard — Doing hard things (18 rungs, from 8, "From about eight; a child and a grown-up side by side")
Order: a ten-minute put-off job now → ten minutes past the fun → a big job split into five steps, step one → the other four within a week → a beginner while watched → a class where you are among the least skilled → ask for an explanation you feel you should not need → get something wrong in front of somebody and retry → one hard thing three days → one planned thing on a low day, then rest → the harder of two options → a safe uncomfortable thing outdoors → yes to one nervous chance this week → a private promise for seven days → go back to something you gave up → help somebody else keep going → write what used to be hard → one hard thing on 28 of 30 days.
Avoided: still1's "hard thing first tomorrow" (rung 3 is splitting a big job instead); "fail publicly" and "be the worst in the room" reworded so nobody is humiliated.
Safety / hard times: defined "hard" as safe things in your control; illness, grief and exhaustion called out as rest days that count (rungs 10, 18 and `about`); weather rung excludes storms, ice and dangerous heat, gives the hot-climate case, child with a grown-up; the nervous-yes rung says never something you have a good reason to refuse.

## belong.story — The story you tell yourself (18 rungs, from 13, "Grown-ups and teenagers")
Order: one "not for people like me" → the prediction, dated and kept → the exact sentence → where it came from (no blame) → one day of noticing the stopping thoughts → evidence for and against → three facts before going (free options) → go for fifteen minutes → what actually happened → a new true sentence → a second, bigger one by the same method → one thing you were right to be careful about and one you were wrong about → read how somebody like you started → ask a person who does it → weekly for four weeks → tell somebody what you want → a third, bigger one → read the first page and tell somebody the old story and what happened (the last rung matches the line).
Kept apart from belong.chair (booking grand visits) by keeping this about the prediction-versus-result method, any ordinary place or activity.
Safety: `about` and rung 1 say pick ordinary safe things; a strong fear or one linked to something that hurt you is to be talked through with somebody trusted, not tested here; rung 8 says leave if you ever feel unsafe; rung 12 says being careful is sometimes right. Examples include a golf club open day and a smart hotel — worth a glance for whether they read as aspirational in the way he wants.
