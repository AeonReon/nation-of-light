# Writer W notes: New experiences, the water ladders (row, fish, sail)

## Shared shape
- Every ladder starts on land (room/home), then a pool or safe edge, and only then the water with a club, instructor or grown-up. `place: "water"` kept on all three (ladder level; there were no rung-level places).
- Safety on every water rung: buoyancy aid or life jacket, an instructor or grown-up, never alone. The fall-in plan is its own rung before the first time in a boat (float first, hold the boat, wait for the instructor), plus a practised capsize with an instructor.
- Kept clear of: water.swim (learning to swim: the pool rungs here are floating IN A BUOYANCY AID and just point to the swimming ladder if you cannot swim ten metres), water.cold (cold shock, not mentioned), wild.weather (forecasts, tides, cold signs: here only the boat-specific wind warnings, offshore wind and gusts), home.safe (plain floating and "never go in after somebody": fishing rung 7 repeats that one line because it is the rule at a bank).

## water.row: Rowing and paddling (17 rungs, `from: 8`; who: From about eight, always with an instructor or grown-up)
Order: choose a boat type, find a club and its taster date, the stroke on the floor with a broom, buoyancy aid lift test, float then swim in one at a pool, the fall-in plan said back before getting in, getting in and out, 20 m out and back, stop and turn on the spot, capsize practice, coming alongside and getting out alone, reading wind and water (go/no-go checked with the instructor), 500 m straight, into a light wind, weekly for four weeks, 5 km with a group, helping a beginner.
Old ladder ended "crossing a lough"; the new line matches the last rung. Old rung "take a beginner out" is now helping at a club session with the instructor still in charge (safer).
Unsure: "stay on the upstream side of the boat in moving water" is standard paddling advice but brief; clubs will say it their way (the note says use their words). Five km at about an hour and a half with a rest is a rough beginner pace.

## water.fish: Fishing (16 rungs, no `from`; who: Everybody; children with a grown-up for hooks and water)
Order: the rules where you live (licence, permit, seasons, size limits, check), the parts, the improved clinch knot on a key ring, the knot on a real hook with a grown-up (barb flattened), casting practice with no hook, eight of ten, the safety rules (never alone, power lines over rods, life jacket on rocks/piers/walls/boats, nobody goes in after anybody, take line home), the five release rules, a net at a pond or rock pool (release practice), watch and ask anglers, one hour fishing, catch and release one, three in an outing, a notebook over five outings, keep and cook one where allowed, teach a beginner.
The release rules are learnt BEFORE the first chance of a catch. Old line "a rock pool to your own supper" replaced; the end is teaching kind catch and release.
Unsure: rung 15 (keeping a fish). The note says a grown-up kills it quickly and correctly as a club shows, never left in a bucket, and gives two alternatives (clean and cook a whole bought fish; or cook for the people you fish with). The owner may prefer to drop the killing rung altogether for a family app. "Power lines conduct through a rod without touching" is the standard angling-body warning; I stated it plainly.

## water.sail: Sailing (20 rungs, `from: 8`; who: From about eight, always with a club or instructor)
Order: parts of a dinghy, find a club and its try-sailing day, reading the wind on a walk, the no-go zone drawn (about 45 degrees either side), figure-of-eight and bowline, buoyancy aid lift test, float then swim in one, the capsize plan said back (stay with the boat), crew and call the wind, rig and check (power lines over masts), tiller and mainsheet across the wind, speed and stopping with the sail, tack, tack between two points three times, capsize practice with a safety boat, reading the wind for a sailing day, gybe (after tacking is steady, light wind), coming back in and stopping, a triangle round three marks, taking somebody on their first sail.
Dropped the old "sail a race" (a club entry, often a fee) and "crew for somebody else" (folded into rung 9). Capsize practice sits after tacking, before gybing, since gybes are the usual cause of a capsize.
Unsure: whether a club would let a three-month sailor take a first-timer out; the rung asks for the club's agreement and a safety boat. "About 45 degrees" for the no-go zone is the usual teaching figure for a dinghy. Sailing is seasonal in cold climates; rung 2 says so.
