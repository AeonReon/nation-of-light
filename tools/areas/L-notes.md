## mind.chess: Chess (20 rungs, no `from`; who: from about seven, child and grown-up side by side)
Order: set-up, the moves, check/checkmate/stalemate on the board, first whole game, castling, the two special pawn moves, piece values, king+queen mate, king+rook mate, opening habits, the "what does it attack?" check, mate-in-one, forks, pins, mate-in-two, notation, five recorded games in a week, review of a lost game, a win against somebody playing properly, teach a beginner. Stalemate is taught before king+queen mate because that is where beginners stalemate by accident. Notation comes before the week of recorded games and the review, which need it.
Unsure: rung 19 (a win) depends on the opponent; the note offers an app one level above your usual. Online play: note says a child plays with a grown-up nearby, chat off. No site names, just "free chess app".

## classic.logic: Spotting a bad argument (20 rungs, `from: 10`)
Order: point + reason, a claim with no reason, then five kinds one per rung (following the crowd, attacking the person, changing the subject, arguing against something nobody said, small step to disaster), a spotting week, five more kinds (only two choices, one case for all, before means because, the wrong expert, a number that does not prove it), a second spotting week, all ten from memory, one in your own words, a good point with a bad reason, a calm question in the moment, "you are right", teach three kinds.
Every example is household, school, shop or sport; nothing political or religious. The only textbook name used is "appeal to popularity" (rung 3); everything else has a plain name.
Unsure: rungs 8 and 14 depend on hearing examples in a week; everyday life gives plenty, but adverts are named as a sure source.

## classic.question: Asking the good question (12 rungs, size weeks; who: from about seven)
Order: "why?" three times alone, closed to open on paper, a real question to a person, the five-second wait, example and non-example of "brave", "what do you mean by...", "how do I know this?", "how did you come to think that?", what a plan takes for granted, "what have we not thought of?" before a group plan, the question others are wondering about in a group, then a fifteen-minute Socratic test of an everyday word ("friend" or "clever") with a `how` list. The ladder is now about the question itself (meanings, how we know, assumptions), leaving listening to speak.listen.
Unsure: rung 11 needs a class, meeting or group to be available; most people have one within a week or two.

## classic.roots: Words and where they come from (20 rungs, `from: 9`)
Order: one root, three Greek roots, five Latin roots, find them around you, six beginnings, five endings, break five words, five more roots, number roots, decode three unknown words, Latin short forms, how to look up an origin, word stories, month names, look-alikes, doctors' words, twenty from memory, other Latin-descended languages, a week of breaking new words, teach. Looking up origins (rung 12) comes before the word stories so the reader can check each story themselves.
Facts, each checked and chosen for being very well known: aqua, tele, phone, scope, micro, port, spect, dict, aud, vis, manu, ped, bio, photo, graph, auto, uni, bi, tri, cent, mille; un/er/less/ful are Old English (said so); e.g./i.e./etc./a.m./p.m./PS; daisy = day's eye, window = Old Norse wind eye, muscle = little mouse, hippopotamus = river horse; September-December from the early Roman year starting in March; island/isle and male/female unrelated; doctor = teacher, patient = sufferer, dens, kardia, pais + iatros (and paediatric "ped" is not Latin "foot"). Deliberately avoided disputed stories (salary and salt, posh, etc.).
Unsure: "centipede, though few have exactly a hundred" is true (pair counts are odd) but kept loose. Old rung "motto on a building or coin" dropped: depends where you live.

## money.run: Running something (20 rungs, `from: 10`)
Order: v57 rung 1 kept exactly; choose one real thing; ask three people; date, times, place and permission; jobs in order; a name against each; one helper; costs or make it free; tell people; three things that could go wrong (with first aid, emergency number, a grown-up in charge for children); reminder the day before; run it; tidy and settle money; thank helpers; ask three people what to change; run it again; set a regular day; give away one job; third run led by somebody else; fourth run with notes somebody else could follow. The four runs are rungs 12, 16, 19, 20, so the far end fits about three months with a weekly or fortnightly thing.
Dropped: "deal with something going wrong" as its own rung (cannot be arranged; now inside rungs 10 and 12), "make it cover its own costs" (inside rung 8), "run something for outsiders".
Unsure: `from: 10` because a child leads with a grown-up present; rung 1 itself suits younger children.

## money.invest: Making money grow (20 rungs, `from: 10`)
Educational only. No product, company, type of investment or crypto is recommended; rung 14 explains a share and says knowing is not a reason to buy; rung 17 is reading one savings account's details, explicitly "reading, not choosing", child with a grown-up, no signing up. `about` says it is not financial advice and points to the country's official financial regulator or a qualified independent adviser.
Order: goal and weeks, savings record, eight weeks of fixed weekly saving (runs in the background), interest, simple vs compound on paper, 10/20/30 years by calculator, rule of 72, older person's prices, the tin and 3 per cent inflation, savings rate vs price rise, 500 owed at 20 per cent vs saved at 4, the two-savers dice game, risk in one sentence, what a share is, what a bank does and deposit protection, what a 1 per cent charge costs over 20 years, reading one account's details, four scam signs plus a regulator warning, saving continued to twelve weeks, teach compound interest and risk.
Numbers checked by calculator: 100 x 1.05^10/20/30 = 162.89 / 265.33 / 432.19; 1.06^12 = 2.012; 100 / 1.03^10 = 74.4; 500 x 1.2^3 = 864; 500 x 1.04^3 = 562.4; 1,000 x 1.05^20 = 2,653.30 vs 1.04^20 = 2,191.12. Dice game: Saver B's average per round is (-10 -5 +0 +5 +10 +15)/6 = 2.50, against A's steady 2.
Unsure: rung 15 on deposit protection: not every country has a scheme, so it says "find out whether". Rung 16 treats a 1 per cent charge as growth of about 4 per cent (exactly 3.95); the note says "about". Rung 11 names store and credit cards generally; no brand.
