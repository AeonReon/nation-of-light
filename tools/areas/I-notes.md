# Writer I notes: the negotiation ladders

## Who owns what (so the six do not repeat each other, or deal.price / nerve.asking / money.value1 / money.handling)
- `deal.price` (already done): paying LESS. Usual price, lower offers, price matching, discounts, quotes, marked goods.
- `deal.more`: getting MORE for the same money. Extras, add-ons, set-up, upgrades, and offering extras yourself instead of a discount.
- `deal.phone`: regular household BILLS AND CONTRACTS, done by phone, email or chat. Yearly cost, contract end dates, new-customer prices, the team for customers thinking of leaving, a calendar of renewals.
- `deal.terms`: everything in a deal that is NOT price or extras. Delivery date, what happens if it goes wrong, returns window, small print, paying in stages, who does the work, getting it in writing, trading one term for another.
- `deal.walkaway`: the LIMIT and SAYING NO. Hurry tactics, your limit and your other option, waiting a day, leaving a shop, turning down add-ons at the till, stopping bidding.
- `deal.both`: the RELATIONSHIP. What the other side wants, saying their side back, what costs you little and is worth a lot to them, truth that costs you, putting right an unfair deal, keeping your word, helping two others agree, a lasting arrangement.
- `money.negotiate`: the whole METHOD for one planned negotiation. "If you..., then I...", preparing (their situation, three numbers, an opening with a reason, a practice run), the moves (name first or ask first, smaller steps, ask something back, a break when tense, say the agreement out loud), a review, then pay/rates, on somebody's behalf, and a big one.
- Rungs I dropped to avoid repeats: "ask then stay silent" (nerve.asking 7), "ask for a better price" (deal.price, nerve.asking 8, money.value1 5), "cancel a subscription" (money.value1 6), "ask for something extra instead of money off" (moved out of money.negotiate into deal.more), "the number you will not go past" (walkaway owns it; negotiate rung 6 refers to it), "swap so both are pleased" and "what they need first" (deal.both only), "walk away" and "bill from your chair" (removed from money.negotiate).

## deal.more: Asking for more instead of less (12 rungs, `from: 10`)
Order: notice what came with a past purchase, ask what comes with it, write three extras you would rather have, then ask for small extras (an extra roll at a stall, then a small extra with a booked service, then delivery set-up or the old one taken away), ask what they can include when price is fixed, ask for one of your three, a better seat or room already paid for, offer extras yourself when somebody asks you for money off, say which extra would make it a yes, and a real purchase with two extras in writing.
Unsure: rung 9 (a better seat or room) only works when you are already paying for one; the note says so. "I would buy it today" must be true; the note says to buy if they agree.

## deal.phone: renamed "Bills by phone and email" (12 rungs, `from: 16`, size kept as "afternoon")
Order: one bill's yearly cost, its end date and leaving fee, the new-customer price, two other companies, a three-sentence script (`how`), contact them, ask for the team for leavers, say honestly you would move (only if true, and then move), a second bill in writing, then every regular payment listed, end dates in a calendar, and one afternoon going through all out-of-contract bills. Checking the next bill is in the note of rung 8 (as its own rung it would have made you wait weeks).
Unsure: the size is "afternoon", but the ladder is really a few sittings. I kept the size as the brief says. Some bills have fixed prices or one supplier in some countries; rung 10 says to leave those out. Safety line on using the number printed on the bill, never one from a message.

## deal.terms: Terms, not just the price (12 rungs, `from: 13`)
Order: noticing terms in a past deal, reading a shop's returns rules, asking about the delivery date, asking what happens if it breaks, reading the small print of something with a sign-up, getting something said confirmed by text, asking who does a job and when, ranking your three terms, paying in stages, a longer returns window, trading a term you care less about for one you care more about, then a job or purchase with all three terms written down before paying.
Unsure: rung 9 (pay in stages) could be read as credit; the note says never borrow or use a credit plan for it. A longer returns window (rung 10) is often refused; the note says a polite no still counts.

## deal.walkaway: Being willing to walk away (12 rungs, `from: 10`)
Order: spot the words meant to hurry you, write your limit, write your other option, an online basket overnight, "I will think about it" in a shop, deciding tomorrow when somebody wants an answer today, saying no when a price is over your limit, turning down an extra at the till, ten minutes away when you feel hurried, no to something you can afford but do not need, stopping bidding at your limit, then no to something you really wanted, looked back on a week later.
Dropped "have somebody come back with a better offer" (nobody can make that happen) and "walk away from somebody you liked" (the relationship part belongs to deal.both). Every "say no" rung says only say it if true: no bluffing.
Unsure: the name "walk away" is a figure of speech. I kept the name because I was only asked to rename deal.phone, and the rung tests use plain words ("leave", "say no"). A plainer name could be "Being ready to say no to a deal".

## deal.both: Both sides pleased (20 rungs, `from: 8`)
Order: a swap you are both glad of, asking what the other person wants first, saying their side back, the orange example (what each wants it for), writing your guesses about a real deal, checking them, offering what costs you little, offering two good options, telling a truth that costs you, a family plan where everybody gets their main thing, saying at the start the one thing you will not do, a deal with somebody you see weekly, checking in a week later, keeping your word when something better came up, putting right an unfair deal, saying no kindly, helping two others agree, a second deal better than the first, asking what would make them glad to deal again, and a regular arrangement running four weeks that both still want.
Unsure: rungs 14 and 15 (keep your word when a better offer comes, put right an unfair deal) wait on something happening; the notes say how to count them. A child really fits only rungs 1–4 and 10; the about says the later rungs suit grown-ups and teenagers.

## money.negotiate: Negotiating (20 rungs, `from: 14`)
Order: what both sides wanted last time, "If you..., then I..." at home, asking why after a no, choosing one real negotiation, the other side's situation, three numbers, an opening with a true reason, a practice run, naming your price first when you sell, asking for their price first when you are quoted, smaller moves, asking for something back, a break when it gets tense, saying the agreement out loud, a three-line review, a price for your own work, a pay rise or better rate, negotiating for somebody else within their limit, a planned one bigger than a week's food shop, and a planned, reviewed one that matters for a year or more.
Unsure: the "name first when you know the price well, ask first when you don't" advice is common teaching, not a proven rule; the notes say it as plain advice, not as fact. Rung 11 (smaller moves each time) is standard and honest as long as you never claim a limit you do not have; the note keeps it to "never go past your limit".
