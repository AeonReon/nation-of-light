# Writer O notes: Home area (keeping, safety, growing, keeping food)

## Who owns what
- `order.keeping` (Keeping it) owns the SYSTEMS that stop mess coming back once a place is right: homes for things, an evening round, a weekly round, one in / one out, post dealt with daily, shared rules and jobs for the household, starting again after a slip. No cleaning technique in it.
- `home.keep` (Keeping a place) owns the HOUSEWORK JOBS themselves, done properly: sweeping, wiping, washing up in order, basin, toilet, hoovering or mopping, laundry basket to drawer, a duvet cover, the bins; then three weekly jobs held for a month, then the house for a day.
- Kept clear of `home.works1`: its week of tidy nights (keeping rung 5 says so and counts it), its one-off paper pile (keeping rung 9 is the daily habit), its first-aid kit build (firstaid rung 3 points to it), its stopcock/fuse box (dropped from home.safe), its plant in a jar (growing rung 8 is a herb cutting potted on). Kept clear of `order.bedroom` (bed making, bedding wash; home.keep does the duvet-cover technique only) and `wild.weather` (cold signs; firstaid does heat only and points there).

## order.keeping: Keeping it (20 rungs, no `from`; who: grown-ups and teenagers, children join the evening round)
Order: homes for the five things, three days using them, move any home that did not work, choose three evening spots and do them once, then seven evenings, a day of putting away at once, one in / one out for a week, write and do the weekly round, post daily for two weeks, the walk-round, the spot that always goes wrong, catching piles at two things, one in / one out for a month, weekly round four weeks, a shared clear surface, evening jobs for everybody, leave rooms right for a week, starting again after a slip, both rounds for a month, three months.
**Extra rungs (final numbers): 1, 10, 11** — all three are the old extras (1, 12, 13) in similar form, with `extra: true` and `mins` (15, 20, 15). Dropped the old "notice putting away has stopped needing a decision" (not a doable test).
Unsure: rungs 15–16 need other people; each note gives the living-alone way to count. Rung 18 waits on a slip; the note says to count it after the next busy weekend.

## home.keep: Keeping a place (12 rungs, `from: 8`; who: from about eight, grown-ups who never learnt too)
Order: the simplest single jobs first (sweep, wipe, wash up), then the bathroom (basin before toilet), a whole floor, laundry in two rungs (sort and wash, then dry, fold, put away), a duvet cover, bins, then three jobs weekly for a month, then the whole house for a day.
Safety line in the basin rung: never mix two cleaning products. A grown-up chooses products and shows the machine the first time.
Unsure: rung 10 says "your local council"; where bins are private or there is no collection, the note is still readable but thin.

## home.firstaid: First aid (20 rungs, `from: 8`; who: grown-ups, and anybody from about eight with a grown-up)
Order: the call (at home, then out), the kit with a card, everyday injuries least to most serious (cut, bleeding, burn, nosebleed, sting and allergy, head bump), heat, stroke FAST, then the unresponsive-person chain (response and breathing, recovery position, fit, choking on a cushion, CPR rhythm on a cushion, finding a defibrillator), a whole pretend emergency, a real hands-on course, first-aider on a day out.
Facts follow mainstream Red Cross / Red Crescent style teaching: burns cool running water at least 20 min, no ice/butter/creams, cling film; nosebleed lean forward, pinch 10 min, help after ~30 min; bee sting scraped out; anaphylaxis signs and helping with their own adrenaline pen; FAST; head/tilt-chin-lift and 10-second breathing check; recovery position; choking up to 5 back blows then up to 5 abdominal thrusts (over one year; babies differ, left to a course); CPR 100–120/min, 5–6 cm on an adult, hands-only if untrained in breaths; fit: time it, nothing in the mouth, 5 minutes. `about` says plainly an app is not a first-aid course, and to follow the course or operator if they differ. Every CPR/choking rung is on a cushion; nothing is pressed on a person except a light hold of a cloth (rung 5) and gentle positioning (12, 13).
Unsure: the head-injury list is condensed and varies a little between countries; "Stayin' Alive" as the beat is widely taught but is a named song. Guidelines differ by country on choking (some teach abdominal thrusts only) — the about's "follow your course" line covers it. Note: apply_v78.py's BAN list includes "999"; the brief for this ladder required "(such as 112, 911 or 999)", so the checker will flag it — that is intended.

## home.safe: Staying safe (12 rungs, `from: 7`; who: children from about seven, with a grown-up)
Order: things known by heart (address, a grown-up's number, the emergency number and when to ring it), then the home (smoke alarms tested, way out and meeting place, a drill with everybody), water (floating in a pool with a lifeguard, then water rules and never going in after somebody), roads (crossing with a grown-up beside, then a known route with the grown-up behind), online rules, and last, showing a younger child the fire plan.
Tone kept calm: each rung is a thing to know, no scare stories. Road note says traffic comes from different sides in different countries. Grown-up does the ceiling alarm reaching.
Unsure: rung 7 needs a pool; somebody far from one cannot do it soon. "Strangers" handled only online (rung 11), as asked; I did not add a stranger-in-person rung because it is hard to write without frightening; say if one is wanted.

## food.grow: Growing (20 rungs, no `from`; who: everybody, side by side)
Order: cress on paper (watch, then eat), a pot of radish or salad, the finger test for watering, spring onions regrown, thinning, a herb cutting, harvesting and sowing again, a month's notebook, one longer crop in a big pot and staking it, then finding your own seasons, learning from a failure, compost, a bigger space with three crops, a dish from one crop, saved seed with a sprout test, giving away. Rungs 1–15 work on a windowsill with no garden.
Seasons: spring given for both halves of the world, plus "where there is no cold season, sow when local growers do"; rung 14 covers frost dates or rainy/dry seasons.
Unsure: the far end is a little over three months (a tomato from seed takes three to four). Sprouted dried lentils are marked "for watching, not for eating raw" (raw sprouts carry a food-poisoning risk).

## food.preserve: Keeping food (20 rungs, no `from`; who: grown-ups and teenagers, children help beside a grown-up)
Order: safest methods first (freezing fruit, labelling, blanching vegetables, freezing a cooked meal within two hours), drying (herbs, apple in the oven), fridge pickles and tasting them, then the "never at home" list BEFORE any jars, then sterilising, jam, a second jam, chutney, sauerkraut and its two weeks, the store list, a lot of one crop kept two ways, a meal from three kept things, jars given away, a store kept three months.
Safety: the never list is low-acid bottling without a pressure canner, garlic/herbs in oil at room temperature, curing meat or fish without a tested recipe, and bulging/leaking/spurting jars. Every jar rung says to follow a trusted source (national food safety agency, university food science service, tested book), including the sealing method (some advise a boiling-water bath). Mould on jam = whole jar out. Sauerkraut: fuzzy/coloured growth, rotten smell or slime = whole jar out. Dropped the old salt/cure rung and kefir/sourdough (sourdough belongs nearer baking).
Unsure: "freezes best within about three months" is a quality guide, not a safety limit; the note says so. Rung 17 waits on a glut; the note gives ways to find one.
