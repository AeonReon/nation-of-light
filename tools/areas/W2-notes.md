# Writer W2 notes: the under-sevens ladder `speak.sayit` — Saying it yourself

`who`: "From about four, with a grown-up beside them" (44 chars). 12 rungs, every one `ctx: "with"`,
`mins: 1` on rung 1. `how` on three rungs only (5, 11, 12); twists on 4, 6, 8, 9, 12.

## The order, and what changed

The old order was sorted by kind of sentence (hello, goodbye, please, thank you, sorry, story). The new
one is sorted by **who the child is speaking to**, because that is what actually makes a rung hard for a
four-year-old, and it runs: people at home → people they know but do not live with → somebody they have
not met → somebody serving them → a room.

1. hello first, at home · 2. a whole sentence with a please · 3. goodbye with the person's name
4. tell somebody at home one thing that happened today · 5. say what you want instead, in words
6. sorry in your own words · 7. hello to somebody you know who does not live with you
8. answer a question yourself · 9. your name to somebody you have not met, grown-up beside
10. thank you to the person serving you · 11. ask for what you want yourself at a counter
12. tell everybody in the room what happened, start to end

- **Rung 1 was "say your name and how old you are to somebody you have not met, looking at them."** That
  is three hard things at once (a stranger, two facts, and eye contact) and it was the first rung. It is
  now rung 9, cut to one fact ("say your name"), with eye contact left out of the test altogether — a shy
  four-year-old can do the speaking or the looking, rarely both, and the speaking is the ladder. The new
  rung 1 is a hello said first to somebody at home: the same move (speak before you are spoken to) with
  the easiest possible audience.
- **Rung 8, "tell somebody you do not want to, in words, instead of crying," is gone.** Its replacement,
  rung 5, is "when you do not want to do something, say in words what you want instead." Crying is not
  named as the wrong answer anywhere, and the note says plainly that crying is not being corrected: you
  wait until the child is calm, then help them put it into words afterwards. The `how` makes it two
  sentences (what they do not want, then what they do want) and puts a duty on the grown-up — answer the
  words seriously, yes or no — because words are only worth using if they are answered.
- **Ordering moved late and is free.** "Order your own food or drink" is now rung 11 and is written as
  "ask for what you want yourself at a counter". The note gives the version that costs nothing for a
  family who never eats out: a book at a library desk, a map at a visitor desk, asking somebody in a big
  building where the toilets are.
- **The story arc is now built, not assumed.** The old ladder asked for a beginning, middle and end at
  rung 11 with no preparation. Rung 4 is the seed (one thing that happened, to somebody at home), its
  twist is the audience jump ("tell it to somebody who was not there"), and rung 12 is the room.
- **One rung dropped:** "ask a grown-up a question you really want the answer to, and listen to all of
  it." It is mostly listening, which is the `speak.listen` ladder's job, and the slot was needed for the
  graded stranger band (name → thank you → counter). The two old rungs about telling a story to one
  person and to somebody who was not there are folded into rungs 4 and 12.

## The grown-up

Rung 1's note carries the rule for the whole ladder, in the plainest words I could find: a child who will
not speak is never pushed — go back a rung, leave it a week, or say the sentence yourself and let them say
it after you, which counts. It is repeated in the practical form at every rung where it bites: say your
own hello and let them copy (1), say the sentence and let them repeat it (2), answer for them without any
comment about it (8), let them see you say your own sorry and leave the rung (6). Two things are banned
outright in the notes: **holding something back until the words come out right** (rung 2) and **asking the
child to say it again so another adult can hear** (rung 9). Rung 10 says stand half a step back and do not
say it again after them; rung 11 says do not repeat the order unless the person truly did not hear; rung 8
says the hard part of that rung is ours, and gives the count of five.

## Safety

The one that matters on this ladder is people the child does not know. Rung 9 (the first such rung) says
it plainly: every rung with somebody they do not know in it is done standing beside the grown-up they came
with, never on their own, and never with somebody the grown-up has only just met unless the grown-up is
right there. Rungs 9, 10 and 11 all name the grown-up in the test or the note.

## Twists

Kept the pattern (a real, harder version of the same rung, spoken to the child): "Tell it to somebody who
was not there" (4), "Say what you will do next time as well" (6), "Give them more than one sentence" (8),
"Ask them their name back" (9), "No ums" (12) — the last one is the twist already used elsewhere for
speaking, and it is the one a grown-up who finds this ladder easy will fail.

## Unsure about

- **Rung 6, saying sorry, at four.** A four-year-old can say "I am sorry I knocked your tower over" when
  they are calm and nobody is standing over them; whether it belongs on a speaking ladder at all is a fair
  question, since half of it is about repair rather than speech. I kept it (it was in the old ladder) and
  wrote the note so that not doing it is an ordinary outcome.
- **Rung 12, "everybody in the room."** Some families will read that as six people and some as two. The
  note says two or three people is a room, and that one listener still counts today with a second added
  next time.
- **Rung 3, goodbye with the person's name.** It is the smallest rung on the ladder. I kept it because
  using a person's name is what rung 7 then needs, and because an easy rung early is worth having.
- **Rung 11's free version** assumes a library or a staffed desk of some kind is reachable. Where there is
  none, the closest thing is asking a market seller what something is called, which I did not put in the
  note because it can look like the start of buying.
