# Writer Z notes: the under-sevens — care.helping and order.ownthings (v81)

## The line I drew between the two ladders
I tested the suggestion and kept it, because it holds all the way up both ladders:

- **care.helping = things done FOR OTHER PEOPLE.** A job that somebody else would otherwise
  have had to do, or a thing given to somebody.
- **order.ownthings = looking after YOUR OWN THINGS.** Your shoes, your coat, your bed, your
  clothes, your books, your toys, your room.

Tidying appears in both on purpose, but on different sides of the line: helping rung 10 is a room
**everybody uses** (kitchen, sitting room, hall — one they did not make untidy on their own),
ownthings rung 11 is **their own room**. The test wording says which is which, and both notes say
it again, so the two never read as the same rung.

### What I moved or replaced
- **care.helping 3 ("Put your own shoes and coat away") is gone.** It was ownthings rungs 1 and 2
  word for word. Replaced by a new rung (now 4) **"Hold a door open and let somebody go through
  first"** — small, public, for somebody else.
- **care.helping 2 ("Put your own plate and cup by the sink") is gone** for the same reason: your
  own plate is your own thing. It became the bigger, clearly-for-others rung 7, **"Clear the table
  with somebody after a meal, and take everybody's things, not only yours"**, which is also a
  proper escalation of rung 1.
- **care.helping: "Fetch something for somebody" moved 6 → 2.** Being told exactly what to fetch is
  the easiest rung on the ladder and belongs at the bottom, next to carrying.
- **care.helping: "Say thank you" moved 11 → 3.** At 11 it sat above helping a smaller child and
  tidying a whole room, which it is nowhere near as hard as. Early, it is also the engine of the
  ladder: you notice somebody doing something for you before you start doing things for them.
- **care.helping 11 is new: "Ask somebody what would help them, then do it."** Every rung before it
  is either told to them or chosen from a list; this is the first time the child goes and finds out
  what is needed, which is what makes rung 12 reachable rather than a jump.
- **order.ownthings: "Do that for a whole afternoon" (old 10) is gone.** It was a weaker copy of the
  last rung and the ladder already escalates once → afternoon → whole day; the afternoon version now
  lives in the note on rung 9, where it costs nothing. Its slot went to a genuinely new skill,
  **"Take something of yours out with you and bring it home again"** (now 10) — keeping track of
  your own things away from home, which is the thing that actually goes missing at that age.
- **order.ownthings 4 lost "without being asked".** The ladder ends with a whole day of nothing
  being asked; having it at rung 4 made the top of the ladder meaningless and the bottom too hard.
- Otherwise ownthings kept its order: it already ran from the things a child handles every day
  (shoes, coat, bed, clothes) through finishing properly (books, lids) to the habit the ladder is
  really for (rung 9, one thing away before the next comes out).

## care.helping — the climb
Told what to do (carry, fetch) → notice what somebody did for you (thank you) → a small thing for a
stranger (door) → a job something depends on (animal or plant), then a week of it → a shared job
(the table) → something made and handed over → helping a smaller child without taking over → a whole
shared room → asking what would help → doing it unasked and unclaimed. `kind` runs mostly
`kindness`, with `attention` on the two noticing rungs and `courage` on 11 and 12.

**Rung 12 and the secret.** The note tells the grown-up to agree beforehand that they are the only
person who ever hears about it, and that at bedtime a nod is enough — the child never has to say
what it was. Then: you will see it before you hear about it (shoes in a row, table set, animal fed),
so say to the room that somebody has done something kind, and say nothing more — do not guess out
loud, do not ask, and do not tell the person it was done for. That is how it gets ticked without
being spoiled.

## order.ownthings — the climb
Shoes → coat → bed → clothes at bedtime → books → tops and lids → tipping a box out and filling it
again (a practice run where the mess is on purpose and nobody is cross) → giving one homeless thing
a home → one thing away before the next comes out → your things brought home again → the whole room
with somebody, to the end → the whole day with nobody reminding you.

**Rung 12 and a missed day.** The note says: tell them in the morning which day it is, then say
nothing until bedtime whatever you see; most days will have a reminder in them somewhere, and a day
with a reminder in it is simply not the day — tomorrow will do, and **nothing goes back to the
bottom of the ladder**. The last line says the day arrives because the earlier rungs have become
ordinary, not because anybody tried harder, so it never reads as a test of effort.

## Keeping it off the chore list
Every rung is a job that really needed doing, and the notes say what the child is allowed to do
badly and finish themselves (two trips with the spoons, the wrong thing fetched, an upside-down
book left for them to find). No rung is framed as what a good child does, nothing warns about what
happens if it is not done, and no note tells the grown-up how their child feels.

## Fields
- `how` only where the doing needs steps: helping 1, 5, 9; ownthings 3, 7, 11.
- `twist` (there were none on either ladder before): helping 1 (two things, one in each hand),
  4 (somebody whose hands are full), 8 (make it for somebody outside your house); ownthings 1
  (line them up so you could step straight into them), 5 (size order), 7 (one hand behind your
  back — the other-hand twist, and the only one here a grown-up would find hard).
- `mins` on rung 1 only: 2 each, as they were.
- `line` rewritten to describe the last rung on both, and `who` added: "From about four, with a
  grown-up beside them" (44 characters). Note that this drops the old "From four, beside you."
  opening from `line`, since `who` now carries it — worth keeping the same across all eight little
  ladders, whichever way it goes.

## Unsure about
- **Helping 5 (feeding an animal).** Safe with the grown-up there, but the amount has to be
  measured out beforehand or a four-year-old will tip the bag in. The note says so, and says an
  animal that is not yours is fed only when its own grown-up says so. A house with no pet and no
  plant gets a cutting in a jar of water, which costs nothing.
- **Helping 7 (clearing the table).** I kept the child on things that cannot break and cannot burn
  and said so in the note. If the owner wants plates in there, it should be a later rung.
- **Helping 9 (a smaller child).** Only a younger brother, sister or cousin, or a friend's child
  while their own grown-up is there — same rule as the adult ladder. Doable at four only with a
  much younger child, and the note names the jobs that work (zips, a shoe, a puzzle piece).
- **Helping 12 at four.** Some four-year-olds will tell everybody within a minute. That is why the
  note gives the bedtime nod: the rung is done whether or not they manage to keep quiet in the
  moment, and nobody is corrected for it.
- **Ownthings 10 (bringing your thing home).** It needs an outing, so it is the one rung here that
  cannot be done in the house. Everything else needs nothing but the home and a reachable hook.
- **Ownthings 7 twist (one hand behind your back).** It is the only twist I am not certain a child
  would enjoy rather than find annoying; drop it if the read says so.
