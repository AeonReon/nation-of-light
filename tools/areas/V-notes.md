## wild.fire: Fire (16 rungs, `from: 12`; who: Grown-ups, and teenagers with a grown-up)
Order: rules and fire danger first (a "no" still counts), a safe spot with water ready, gather, sort into three piles, lay unlit and have it checked, strike matches over water at home, light and feed, put out cold, leave no trace, keep one going thirty minutes, then the harder lighting: one match, wind (with "strong wind = don't light"), after rain, fire striker; then run a fire for an evening for others, then teach somebody.
Safety lines: three metres clear, never on peat or deep leaves, never burn plastic/foil, somebody always with a lit fire, back-of-hand then touch for cold, anybody under 16 not stated as a number: I used "teenagers with a grown-up beside them at every lit fire" throughout.

## food.firecook: Cooking on fire (14 rungs, `from: 12`; who: Grown-ups, and teenagers with a grown-up)
Order: toast on a stick, waiting for embers with the hand-count test, clean hands and raw/cooked kept apart, bread twist, foil parcel, keeping steady heat, sausages cooked through, a steady pot, boiling water, one-pot meal for two, two things at once, washing up away from water, meal for six, the whole day out. A charcoal barbecue counts for every rung.
Safety/fact lines: "food-safety guides generally say about 75 degrees in the middle" (attributed, not stated as settled; some countries' guides say 70 for 2 minutes or 74); use metal or a known-tree stick because some woods are poisonous; no stones from rivers/shore (can crack); pot on metal, not a wooden stick.
Unsure: the hand-count heat test is a rough rule of thumb; worded as such.
