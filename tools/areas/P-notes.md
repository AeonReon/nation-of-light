## make.knots: Knots and rope (12 rungs, size weeks, no `from`; who: from about seven, side by side)
Order: stopper knot (figure of eight), reef vs granny, clove hitch, round turn and two half hitches with a load, bowline, bowline round your own waist, sheet bend, taut-line hitch (uses the bowline), all seven blind, whipping a rope end, square lashing, a braced square frame of five sticks.
Old "a structure that holds a person" and "undo a hard-loaded knot" dropped: the far end is now a braced frame, and the about says these knots are never for climbing or holding a person's weight.
Unsure: taut-line hitch description is condensed (two turns inside the loop, one outside); a picture would help. Rung 11 needs sticks or canes (kit).

## make.clay: Clay and carving (18 rungs, no `from`; who: from about five; knife rungs teens with a grown-up)
Order: pinch pot, wall check by cutting in half, coil, score-and-wet join (old note kept and expanded), coil pot, slow drying, slab tile, pulled-out animal, second animal better, paint and seal, carve lines into half-dry clay, slab box, lidded pot; then carving: a clay block carved subtractively (the bridge, child-safe), whittling (grown-ups / teens with a grown-up), butter spreader, sanding, oiling and using it.
FIXED a factual problem: the old ladder had "a pinch pot that holds water", "a bowl you eat from" and "fire or bake so it lasts" — air-drying clay is not waterproof or food-safe even sealed, so those are gone and rung 10 says so.
Unsure: younger children cannot really do rungs 15-16 themselves; the notes give them a clay alternative (15) and a grown-up doing the knife cuts while they mark, sand and oil (16-18). If the owner wants the far end fully child-doable, it would have to end on the lidded pot instead. Salt-dough recipe given as "about" one cup of water.

## make.cloth: Cloth and thread (18 rungs, no `from`; who: from about seven, side by side)
Order: thread and knot, running stitch, back stitch, finishing off, button, seam, filled pouch (first whole object), pressing a seam, hem, blanket stitch on fleece, measuring and cutting a rectangle, drawstring bag (channel = training for the elastic waist), tracing a pattern from a pillowcase, cushion cover with envelope back, mending for somebody at home, altering your own clothes, tracing shorts/skirt, sewing and wearing them.
All by hand: the old machine rung and zip rung dropped (no purchase; zips belong to home.mend). Darning dropped (home.mend has it). Iron is a grown-up's job for younger children, with finger-pressing as the no-iron way.
Unsure: shorts from a traced pattern are the hardest jump (rung 17-18); the skirt as a gathered rectangle is the easy route. Elastic is a small purchase with no free alternative named (a drawstring from rung 12 would work; could add).

## make.wood: Wood (18 rungs, `from: 8`; who: from about eight with a grown-up; hand tools only)
Order: sanding, grain and grit, measuring and marking square, first saw cut (clamped, grown-up beside), straight square cut, three equal pieces, hand drill, pilot hole and screw, glue and clamp, plan a box, build the box (mistake list), chisel paring, sharpening, halving joint, mending a mistake, oil/wax finish, stool plan, build and stand on the stool.
Hand tools only; no power drill anywhere (home.tools owns that). Safety lines: clamp always, both hands behind the chisel, avoid painted and pallet wood, oily-rag self-heating warning on linseed-type finishes, a grown-up checks the stool before anybody stands on it.
Unsure: stool design is described in one sentence; it is sturdy enough on paper at 20-25 cm but a grown-up check is the real safety line. Rung 13 sharpening is written for grown-ups/teens with younger children watching.

## words.callig: Beautiful writing (16 rungs, no `from`; who: from about nine, side by side)
Order: two taped pencils (thick and thin), guide lines at five pen-widths, one pen angle on o and n (old note kept in plainer form), italic by letter family (straight, arched, oval, the rest), a week of practice, spacing with "minimum", capitals, first quotation, circle three faults and rewrite, page plan, decorated capital, a name card given, the final framed page.
Chose italic only (cheap pen, readable, quick). Dropped copperplate, "second hand", envelope and public sign rungs (posting costs; a public sign is hard to arrange anywhere). Kept clear of words.hand.
Unsure: letter-family groupings follow common italic teaching (Getty-Dubay style); "u like an upside-down n" is a simplification. The clock-hand image for the slant is literal, not a figure of speech, but check it reads plainly.

## make.photo: Photography (17 rungs, no `from`; who: from about six, side by side)
Order: clean lens and hold still, tap to focus, get closer (old note kept), window light side vs behind, plain background, grid lines, three heights, low light with the phone rested, low sun vs midday, movement, a person at home (ask first), best three of twenty on a walk (things not people), light edit keeping the original, a week of one photo a day, a family occasion, print and give, a show of your ten best.
People rules as briefed: ask before photographing anybody, never photograph children you do not know, other people's children only with their parents' say. The walk rung is explicitly things and places. Printing has a free alternative; the show can be on a laptop or television. Hemisphere/latitude line on the sunrise rung.
Unsure: "photo counters" and "chemists" as places to print vary by country; worded as "many ... print from a phone for a small price".
