## mind.tables: Times tables (20 rungs, `from: 7`)
Order: count in tens and twos, then the ten, two and five tables, twenty mixed from those, then a 12 by 12 grid showing only 36 facts are left, then three, four (double double), eight (double three times), twenty mixed, nine (ten lots take one), six (five lots and one), eleven and twelve, and seven last because by then only 7 times 7 is new. Then the grid from memory, ten harder facts, thirty mixed, thirty with a three-second limit from somebody else, teach a table, and fifty mixed in under three minutes.
Changed the far end: the old "say every table up to twelve in sixty seconds" is 144 facts in a minute, which is not possible out loud. Fifty questions in three minutes is hard but reachable.
Unsure: some countries' schools stop at ten times; I kept twelve (the old ladder did). For a seven-year-old starting from nothing, three months is ambitious; for an older child or a grown-up filling gaps it is about right.

## mind.mental: Mental arithmetic (20 rungs, `from: 8`)
Order: single-digit adding, take-away inside twenty by counting up, crossing a ten, two-digit adding, rounding and putting right, two-digit take-away, four numbers, doubling and halving, two-digit times one-digit, times 5 and 9 via ten lots, dividing, percentages, rough answers first, two-digit times two-digit, then out into real shopping (running total, change, splitting a receipt, better value per 100 g), teach one method, and a final twenty mixed sums set by somebody else in under ten minutes, at least eighteen right.
Plain numbers only, no currency symbols. The running-total rung says what to do if local prices are big whole numbers (aim within 100).
Unsure: "within 1 of the till" assumes prices with two decimal places; the note covers the other case. The value rung uses grams, which is fine almost everywhere.

## mind.codes: Codes and ciphers (12 rungs, `from: 8`)
Order: shift of three (with `how`), a shift message to somebody, reading a shift cipher with no key (given in the note), keyword alphabet, counting letters, reading somebody's mixed-up alphabet using the counts, book cipher, Morse for your name, first-letter hidden message, a keyword that changes the shift for each letter, a cipher of your own, and a treasure hunt of five clues in five different codes.
Letter-frequency note says E, T, A, O, I is for English and other languages differ. Morse note has a safety line: never signal SOS outside for fun.
Checked by code: MEET AT FOUR -> PHHW DW IRXU; AOL JHRL PZ PU AOL ISBL APU is shift 7 (THE CAKE IS IN THE BLUE TIN); GARDEN alphabet and HELLO -> CEJJM; CAT keyword, SEE YOU AT NOON -> UEX AON CT GQOG. "Caesar is said to have used" and "once called the cipher nobody could read" are both worded as reported, not as fact.

## mind.cube: The cube (20 rungs, `from: 8`)
Order: pieces and opposite colours, the six letters (with `how`), R U R' U' six times returns the cube, daisy, white cross, one white corner, the white layer, white layer three times from memory, middle edge right, middle edge left, first two layers, first two layers three times, yellow cross, yellow edges, yellow corners into place, twist the corners and first full solve with notes, three solves with notes, one solve from memory, three solves under five minutes each, and a mix-up from somebody else in under three minutes.
Seven sequences exactly, matching the `about`: R U R' U'; U R U' R' U' F' U F; U' L' U L U F U' F'; F R U R' U' F'; R U R' U R U2 R'; U R U' L' U R' U' L; R' D' R D. The `about` says the cube costs little and can often be borrowed.
Checked on a simulator: (R U R' U') six times = solved; a corner goes in within five goes; both middle-edge sequences put the lined-up edge into the right/left gap and keep the rest of the first two layers; F R U R' U' F' takes dot -> L (front-right) -> hold at back-left -> line -> hold left-right -> cross, keeping the first two layers; R U R' U R U2 R' then U solves edges when the matching pair is at back and right, and from an opposite pair it gives an adjacent pair; U R U' L' U R' U' L keeps the front-right-top corner fixed; (R' D' R D) twice twists that corner, and six times is the identity.
Unsure: rung 16's "twist that one corner round by hand" is for a cube that was put back wrongly; it is safe on most cubes but can pop a piece on a stiff one. Under three minutes from memory is a fair beginner-method target after three months, but some people will take longer.
