# Writer C notes

## wild.weather: Weather and tide (20 rungs, everybody, shore rungs with a grown-up)
Order: dress for what you feel at the door, then read a forecast (hour by hour, check it against the sky at the same three times, "feels like", rain radar, warnings), then daylight, layers, drying out, signs of cold, looking after others, a turn-back time. Then the tide: table first, the four shore rules second, then watching the water at the shore and a planned shore walk (the only two `place: "coast"` rungs, since looking up times and learning the rules need no coast). Then the inshore forecast, changing a plan, deciding the plan for others, and leading a wet walk.
Kept clear of `sky.wind` (cloud types, wind direction, predicting from the sky, sunset from memory) and `wild.door1` (reading tomorrow from tonight's sky, packing a day bag).
Unsure: that the Met Office inshore waters forecast has a Northern Ireland section (I believe "Lough Foyle to Carlingford Lough"; the rung does not name it). The first-aid wording follows the NHS signs list but is paraphrased, so check it. "Wet shiny sand above the waterline often means a falling tide" is a rough rule, and I kept "often". Rung 18 ("the next time the forecast turns against a plan") waits on a real plan; I could not find a way to make it happen now without making one up.

## sky.stars: Stars and planets (20 rungs, everybody, a grown-up with a child in the dark)
Order: things that never set from 54–55°N first (Plough, North Star, Cassiopeia, Mizar and Alcor), then dark eyes, drawing from memory, the sky turning, then one seasonal pattern (the note covers all months: Orion Nov–Mar, Arcturus Apr–Jul, Summer Triangle Aug–Oct). Then a planet, the space station, shooting stars, the Moon through binoculars, the next season's stars, why they change. Then plan a dark-site trip, the Milky Way, Andromeda, ten things alone, show one person, lead an hour.
Dropped "moon phase and where it will be tomorrow" and "first star at dusk" (both in `wild.door1`).
Facts I am confident of: the Plough, Cassiopeia and Vega never set here; pointers about 5x the gap; the North Star sits at about your latitude; the sky turns anticlockwise about the North Star; stars rise about 4 min earlier a night; Perseids ~12 Aug, Geminids ~14 Dec; Venus only near the Sun; Davagh Forest is a Dark Sky Park.
Less sure, worth a look: the Andromeda star-hop wording (from Alpheratz, two stars along to Mirach, turn towards Cassiopeia past mu Andromedae); "Its light left about two and a half million years ago" (the usual figure). Rung 9 (a planet) and rung 10 (space station) depend on what is up that week; both notes say to skip ahead and come back. Rungs 16–17 depend on the season (Milky Way best Aug–Oct evenings, Andromeda Aug–Feb evenings), and the notes say so.

## calm.gratitude: Noticing the good (12 rungs, everybody, a family can climb it together)
Order: one good thing said out loud, then with a detail, then three days, then about a person, thanking at home, thanking outside, finding good in a hard day, a family jar for a week, a letter by post, the jar for thirty days, reading it back, starting it at another table.
Stayed clear of `calm.still1`: no "three things written tonight", no thanking somebody for something long ago, no "a week of things given for nothing", no face-to-face big thank-you, no secret kindness. The jar is my replacement for "write three each night for a week".
Unsure: rung 7 (a hard day this month) should be fine for most people, but a person going through something very heavy may find it too much. Nothing on the ladder says so.

## calm.attention: Attention (20 rungs, `from: 12`, grown-ups and anybody from about twelve)
Order: ten minutes → a written aim → phone away → counting drifts → 25 min → notifications off → a "later" list → finishing one at a time → two 25s → find the top three pulls → remove one → 40 min difficult reading → 1 hour no screen → same time three days → a boring task to the end → 90 min → 2 hours → 3 hours once → teach somebody → three hours weekly for three weeks.
Took out "hard thing first" and "meal with no screen" (both in `calm.still1`). Swapped the last rung from "notice what you can now do" to the weekly three-hour block so the far end is a real, measurable thing.
Unsure: whether `from: 12` is right. A ten-year-old could do the early rungs with homework, but the three-hour blocks are not sensible below about twelve.

## nerve.asking: Asking out loud (12 rungs, `from: 10`, a grown-up close by for any ask of a stranger)
Order: asking somebody to repeat something (lowest stake), free small things in a café, a shop checking the back, a hand from a stranger, a question in front of a group, a swap, the silent pause after an ask, a better price, a favour from somebody you know, half an hour of somebody's time, the real ask, standing beside somebody else's first ask.
Left out "the time" and "directions" and "a recommendation", which are rungs in `speak.people1`, so the two ladders do not repeat.
Unsure: the carrier-bag charge in NI means a bag is not free, so I left bags out. The price rung includes broadband and phone renewals; asking for a better deal on those is common in the UK, but results vary.

## belong.chair: From where you are sitting (12 rungs, `from: 14`, grown-ups and teenagers)
Order: look up a grand building's times → find a free way in → real prices of fine things → coffee or afternoon tea in a grand hotel → a night in the best hotel → a written list of three places with one fact each → email one → phone another → book something free → diary and travel → tell one person → book the visit you most doubted. Every rung except "tell one person" is `room`. The ladder ends with booking the visit, not going on it. The going is in `belong.rooms`, `belong.shops` and `belong.cars`.
Unsure: (1) The name "From where you are sitting" is also a common phrase meaning "in my opinion". I kept it because the brief says to keep names unless they are wrong, but it may break the no-idioms rule, so it is his call. (2) Facts in the notes: Belfast City Hall free public tours (confident); Stormont Parliament Buildings free tours (I wrote "has run", so check it); European Heritage Open Days in September in NI (confident). The golf-club and £4 coffee examples are written as the kind of thing a reader might note down, not as claims about a particular place.
