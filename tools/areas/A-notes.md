# Writer A notes

## speak.listen — Listening (17 rungs, from 8, for everybody)
Order: the plain physical habits first (phone down, silence, let them finish), then a radio rung as a no-person rehearsal for saying back, then saying back to a person, questions instead of stories, five minutes of questions, noticing when you have lost somebody. Then sustained attention (a thirty-minute talk), then the emotional rungs (the second "how are you", upset without advice, "help or listen?"), then disagreement (listen through, say their side back, say both sides of a small family disagreement), and last coaching: ten minutes on a small problem, twenty minutes on a real decision.
Avoided: people1's two-minute listen (rung 7 names it and goes longer, with somebody known); care.older's hour with an older person was dropped from this ladder.
Unsure: rung 15 (saying both sides of two people's disagreement) for an eight-year-old is sibling-level only; the note limits it to small things. Nearly every rung is `with` by nature; rungs 4 and 9 are `room`.

## care.giving — Giving (18 rungs, everybody, side by side)
Order: at home and small (share, bigger half, a job done quietly, half an hour, a thing you still like, something made, receiving well, anonymous kindness, giving up your turn), then a chosen weekly amount (20p pocket money up to a few pounds; an hour of time if money is tight), then a local group: ask what they need, give exactly that, an hour of time, a collection from three people, deliver and report back. Then four weeks of weekly giving, one person invited, and twelve weeks with somebody alongside.
Avoided: old rungs that were abstract ("give a chance rather than a thing", "without deciding whether they deserved it"). care.helping's "helpful before asked" is close to rung 3, but rung 3 is specifically somebody else's usual job, done unmentioned.
Unsure: rung 14 (a collection box at work or school) depends on permission; the note says the teacher's say-so.

## speak.strangers — Talking to people you do not know (18 rungs, from 8, children with a grown-up beside them)
Order: people at work only, never random strangers, so it is safe for a child and does not repeat people1. Order at a counter, ask for something not on show, one off-topic question, somebody working in a public place, two linked questions, advice on a real problem, go back and report, a long-time shopkeeper's story. Then the interview spine: write ten questions, test on somebody known, three questions on the spot, a booked ten-minute interview, a written copy given back, a twenty-minute interview with somebody whose work is unknown to you, ask "who else should I talk to?", and last a half-hour interview with that introduced stranger.
Avoided: people1's hello/time/directions/shop question/compliment/phone rungs; people2's introductions; care.welcome's "the one standing alone". Old child-only rungs (pay for something, teach another child to order) gone.
Unsure: whether the name should become something like "Asking people what they know"; I kept it because the rungs still are talking to people you do not know.

## care.place — Your own patch (17 rungs, everybody, side by side)
Order: choose a small public place (away from fast traffic), survey it, clear it, sort the rubbish, report what you cannot fix, clear it again a week later, find the owner, chase the report, a non-litter job, four weeks, one helper, ask permission to plant, plant, arrange council collection ahead of a group tidy, three helpers, report back, twelve weeks. Permission now comes before planting (the old ladder planted first and asked at rung 16).
Avoided: town1's ten-minute litter street; rung 3's note names it and says this is the same job in your own place, repeated.
Unsure (facts not verified at source): that roads and street lights can be reported through nidirect; that many councils collect bags from volunteer clean-ups and lend pickers; that Keep Northern Ireland Beautiful supports local clean-ups (its Live Here Love Here programme, not named in the rung). Safety: needles/glass never touched, a grown-up checks before a child picks anything up.

## money.handling — Handling money (16 rungs, from 7, child with pocket money and grown-up side by side)
Order: coins in order, a pound three ways, adding three prices in your head, pay cash and count change, change from £5 (play shop), change from £10/£20 in your head, price per 100g, the bigger pack that is dearer per 100g, checking a week of card payments, a week of money in and out, sorting it into needed/wanted/not noticed, an envelope week, one meal planned and priced, that meal shopped under budget, a whole week's shop planned, and last that shop done under budget with the receipt checked. Every rung's note gives the grown-up version or the child version.
Avoided: value1 rung 1 (a day of spending) is extended to a week and the note says so; no tenth-aside, no no-spend day, no selling.
Unsure: rung 9 (card payments) for a child means looking at a grown-up's statement with them; a family may not want that, so the rung says the child finds three of the grown-up's payments, which the grown-up controls.

## deal.price — Asking for a better price (14 rungs, from 10, grown-ups and teenagers; a child with own pocket money with a grown-up beside them)
Order: know the price before you ask (your guess, three real prices, what somebody else paid), rehearse the sentence, say a number on something second-hand, agree a number between yours and theirs, write down the words that worked, then shops: price match, two or more, discounts, marked goods, two written quotes for a job, ask who can decide, and last money off a printed price tag. Every rung is on something you were buying anyway, so no extra money; honesty line on price matching.
Avoided: value1's "Is that your best price?" (rung 5 here is saying an actual number instead); deal.more (extras), deal.phone (bills), deal.walkaway and money.negotiate ("ask, then stop talking") not repeated.
Unsure: rung 12 (written quotes for a job) is really a grown-up rung; the note lets a teenager make the calls. Price matching is shop by shop; the note says "some large shops".
